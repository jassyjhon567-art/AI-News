Early iPhone Duo Hands-On Videos Reveal Familiar Foldable Display Challenges

9to5google.com · Wed, 09 Sep 2026 22:10:00 +0000

Early hands-on videos of the rumored iPhone Duo indicate that Apple faces familiar foldable hardware hurdles, including the persistent display crease seen in rival Android devices.

Apple has long maintained a reputation for exercising patience before entering emerging hardware categories, often waiting for competitors to navigate early technical hurdles and refine unproven concepts. Historically, the company enters the market only after engineering solutions are mature enough to meet its exacting standards. However, recent hands-on videos circulating online of the rumored iPhone Duo suggest that creating a flexible smartphone presents physical obstacles that even Cupertino cannot entirely avoid.

The preview footage highlights several design compromises that have challenged Android manufacturers for years. Most notably, the device appears to feature a noticeable crease along the hinge line where the screen folds inward. Android pioneers like Samsung and Google have spent multiple product generations attempting to eliminate this indentation with minimal success. While some industry watchers hoped Apple would deliver an entirely flat, crease-free flexible panel, these early looks indicate the laws of materials science and hinge physics remain restrictive.

Despite ongoing advancements in ultra-thin glass and internal hinge mechanisms, folding displays continue to require structural trade-offs. For prospective buyers, the visible and tactile presence of a screen crease has long been one of the primary deterrents to adopting foldable phones. If Apple decides to move forward with a commercial rollout featuring these visible compromises, the company will have to rely heavily on software optimization, durability guarantees, and distinct ecosystem advantages to persuade consumers that the device justifies its expected premium pricing.
