UK Government Warned Over Data Centers' Massive Water Demands Amid Expansion Plans

www.techradar.com · Sat, 25 Jul 2026 06:00:00 +0000

Industry body Water UK has warned the government that expanding data center capacity by 2030 without managing their immense water cooling needs could severely strain national water resources.

Industry trade body Water UK has raised significant concerns regarding the British government's apparent oversight of water consumption within the rapidly expanding technology sector. As the nation prepares to scale up its digital infrastructure to power modern enterprise, the trade group warns that ministers have largely forgotten that high-performance data centers require substantial volumes of water to cool their server systems. Without immediate strategic planning and regulatory oversight, this growing appetite for resources could place severe, unprecedented strain on local water supplies and municipal infrastructure throughout the country.

The trade body's warning comes at a critical juncture, particularly as the UK government actively pursues ambitious plans to treble national data center capacity by the year 2030. These digital hubs are considered essential for supporting the rapid rise of artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and advanced digital services. However, industry experts caution that operating these massive facilities requires continuous, intensive cooling systems. Water UK argues that expanding digital capacity without thoroughly evaluating regional water availability risks triggering severe localized shortages and long-term environmental degradation.

To prevent potential resource crises, Water UK is urging government officials to establish tighter regulations and collaborate closely with water utility companies prior to approving new development projects. The trade association recommends promoting closed-loop cooling systems, incentivizing the use of recycled or non-potable water, and strategically placing new data facilities in regions with resilient water supplies. Ensuring that the UK's digital ambitions do not compromise its vital natural resources will require a balanced, forward-looking approach to infrastructure development.
