Why People Are More Willing to Share Failures Than Successes

www.inc.com · Sun, 13 Sep 2026 13:22:33 -0400

A study indicates people often share personal failures over successes to protect others' feelings, using vulnerability to foster empathy, connection, and social harmony.

Traditional assumptions often suggest that individuals eagerly jump at every opportunity to showcase their triumphs, yet the reality of human conversation reveals a far more complex dynamic. While achievements are undeniably celebrated in society, people frequently exhibit noticeable caution when it comes to publicly disclosing their personal successes. In stark contrast, stories of missteps, vulnerability, and outright failure emerge far more naturally in everyday discourse, prompting researchers to look deeper into the underlying social motivations behind our disclosures.

A comprehensive behavioral study involving roughly 8,000 participants sheds light on how interpersonal sensitivity fundamentally governs our conversational choices. Rather than seeking self-promotion at all costs, people tend to carefully evaluate the emotional climate and perceived feelings of their listeners before opening up. Flaunting high achievements can inadvertently provoke envy, discomfort, or feelings of inadequacy in others. Consequently, many individuals consciously downplay their victories or withhold good news to protect social harmony and avoid being perceived as arrogant.

Conversely, discussing failure acts as a powerful relational bridge, creating instant rapport and psychological safety between individuals. Admitting setbacks signals genuine humility, lowers social defenses, and reassures peers that everyone grapples with hardship. The findings ultimately suggest that disclosure is an inherently empathetic process; rather than focusing solely on personal pride, people intuitively calibrate their conversations to comfort and connect with the person right in front of them.
