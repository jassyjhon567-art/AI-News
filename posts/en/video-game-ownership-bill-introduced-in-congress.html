New US Bill Aims to Protect Consumer Ownership of Digital Video Games

www.theverge.com · 2026-10-18T12:27:51-04:00

A newly introduced US congressional bill directs the Federal Trade Commission to establish regulations guaranteeing that consumers own the video games they purchase rather than merely licensing them.

United States Representative Ritchie Torres of New York has officially introduced new federal legislation designed to reform and strengthen consumer rights within the rapidly growing video game industry. Formally titled the "Power To The Players Act," the proposed bill directly tackles modern digital distribution models. Its central objective is to ensure that customers who spend money to buy video games receive authentic legal ownership over their purchases, rather than being relegated to restrictive and conditional licensing contracts that can be altered at any time.

If successfully enacted into law, the bill would instruct the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) to establish comprehensive regulatory frameworks protecting digital buyers across all major gaming platforms. In recent years, video game enthusiasts have encountered frequent frustrations with online marketplaces, where publishers hold the unilateral power to amend service terms, restrict account access, or shut down online systems entirely. Because modern transactions are commonly treated as non-permanent software licenses, consumers frequently discover that paying full retail price does not confer the durable property rights they had expected.

The legislative initiative comes at a pivotal time when digital downloads and subscription platforms have largely replaced traditional physical media, such as cartridges and discs. Advocates for consumer protection have repeatedly cautioned that current laws have failed to keep pace with this digital transition, leaving buyers vulnerable to losing access to their content collections without compensation or recourse. By pursuing federal intervention, the proposed legislation seeks to establish clearer transparency and ensure that player investments in digital libraries remain permanently protected.
