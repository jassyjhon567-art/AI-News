Diagnostic Test for Alzheimer's Could Help Identify CTE in Living Patients

www.sciencenews.org · Fri, 31 Jul 2026 17:00:00 +0000

Emerging research suggests that diagnostic tests designed for Alzheimer's disease may help detect Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy in living patients by identifying shared protein biomarkers.

Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE), a devastating neurodegenerative condition linked to repeated head impacts, has long evaded early detection. Traditionally, a definitive diagnosis could only be achieved posthumously through brain autopsies. However, emerging scientific research indicates that advanced diagnostic testing developed for Alzheimer’s disease may help identify signs of CTE in living patients, marking a potentially major breakthrough for neurology and sports medicine.

The connection lies in the underlying molecular biology of both disorders, particularly the accumulation of specific abnormal proteins in the brain. CTE is characterized by the toxic buildup of tau protein, which distorts brain tissue and leads to severe memory loss, mood instability, and cognitive impairment. Because Alzheimer’s disease also involves tau tangles alongside amyloid plaques, diagnostic techniques designed to detect these biomarkers—such as specialized blood tests and sophisticated imaging scans—are showing promise in flagging CTE pathology early on.

While these findings are still in their early stages, the implications for athletes participating in contact sports and military personnel exposed to repeated blast injuries are profound. Detecting CTE during a patient's lifetime could allow clinicians to intervene earlier with therapeutic strategies, lifestyle modifications, and targeted clinical trials. Researchers emphasize that further studies are necessary to refine these tests, but utilizing existing Alzheimer's diagnostic infrastructure could significantly accelerate the quest for a reliable living diagnosis.
