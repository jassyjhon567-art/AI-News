Canadian Cybercriminal Pleads Guilty to Massive Snowflake Data Extortion Scheme

krebsonsecurity.com · Thu, 06 Aug 2026 17:00:56 +0000

A 26-year-old Canadian hacker pleaded guilty to federal cybercrime charges after breaching over 165 Snowflake-connected organizations and stealing data from over 100 million AT&T customers.

A 26-year-old Canadian man recognized as one of the most significant cybercrime threat actors of 2024 has officially pleaded guilty to federal charges involving computer fraud and extortion. Connor Riley Moucka, residing in Kitchener, Ontario, admitted to his role in a massive hacking campaign that targeted over 165 organizations utilizing the cloud-based data storage provider Snowflake. His admission marks a major breakthrough for international law enforcement agencies working to dismantle high-profile cybercrime operations.

According to court documents and prosecutors, Moucka used compromised credentials to infiltrate corporate systems, exfiltrate sensitive data, and demand lucrative ransom payments under the threat of leaking proprietary information online. Beyond the widespread attacks on Snowflake customers, Moucka also confessed to illegally accessing and stealing the call and text history records of more than 100 million AT&T subscribers. The sheer scale of the compromised data positioned this breach among the most severe corporate security incidents recorded in recent years.

Legal experts note that the successful prosecution of Moucka underscores growing cross-border cooperation between North American cyber authorities in apprehending dangerous hackers. Moucka now faces substantial prison time and financial penalties as part of his plea agreement. The incident has served as a critical wake-up call for major cloud service clients and telecommunications providers, prompting widespread reviews of authentication measures, credential management, and internal data security protocols across the industry.
