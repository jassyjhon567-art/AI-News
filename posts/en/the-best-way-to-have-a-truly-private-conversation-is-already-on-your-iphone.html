How to Use Apple's Notes App for Completely Private Messaging

www.cnet.com · 2022-06-17T13:00:02Z

Apple's built-in Notes app offers iPhone users a highly effective and discreet way to hold private conversations through its real-time document collaboration features.

In an era where digital privacy is increasingly difficult to maintain, many smartphone users are seeking alternative methods to communicate securely. While end-to-end encrypted messaging applications like Signal and WhatsApp remain popular choices, a surprisingly effective and discreet tool for private conversations is already built into every iPhone: the Apple Notes app. By clever usage of the application's built-in document collaboration features, users can communicate in real time without leaving a traditional messaging paper trail.

To initiate a secret conversation, a user simply creates a new document within the Notes app and shares access with another person using their Apple ID or phone number. Once the recipient accepts the invitation, both participants can type messages back and forth inside the shared canvas. To maintain maximum privacy, users can delete text immediately after reading or overwrite previous statements continuously. Because this activity occurs within a collaborative cloud document rather than a standard messaging network, it effectively bypasses traditional chat logs and notification previews.

Furthermore, the Notes app provides essential security layers, including password protection and biometric locking via Face ID or Touch ID, ensuring the document remains safe from unauthorized physical access. While this method may not entirely replace dedicated communication platforms for everyday banter, it offers an exceptionally clever and pre-installed solution for moments when discreet communication is essential.
