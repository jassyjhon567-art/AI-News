Birds Play a Vital and Overlooked Role in Circulating Earth's Nutrients, Study Finds

phys.org · Mon, 19 Oct 2026 18:00:01 EDT

A comprehensive scientific review reveals that birds play an indispensable role in global ecosystems by transporting vital nutrients across oceans, forests, and land through their waste and bodies.

Birds play a far more critical and outsized role in shaping the planet's chemistry than has been traditionally appreciated. According to an extensive new scientific review, these winged creatures act as fundamental conduits for essential nutrients, systematically transporting vital elements across vast distances that connect open oceans, continental forests, and fragile coral reefs. Their high mobility allows them to bridge disparate ecosystems, actively transferring organic and inorganic resources from nutrient-rich foraging zones to nutrient-poor environments, thereby sustaining life far beyond their immediate nesting sites.

The comprehensive study, published on October 19 in the journal Current Biology, was conducted by a team of researchers from Cornell University in collaboration with scientists from five other academic institutions. By systematically reviewing and synthesizing findings from nearly 400 published scientific papers, the research team detailed how birds constantly redistribute key elements across land, lakes, and seas. This dynamic transfer occurs primarily through their metabolic waste, food provisioning for chicks, molted feathers, and physical remains.

This ongoing ecological transport exerts a profound influence on global environmental systems, directly regulating soil fertility, water chemistry, and primary biological productivity. The steady influx of bird-deposited nutrients stimulates plant growth, invigorates microbial life, and supports diverse animal populations in otherwise barren locations. From enriching coastal islands with marine-derived nitrogen to fertilizing remote rainforest canopies, birds serve as indispensable environmental engineers. The authors highlight that safeguarding avian populations is therefore crucial not only for biodiversity, but also for maintaining the vital geochemical cycles that support life across the globe.
