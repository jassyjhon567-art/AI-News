CDC Warns of Salmonella Outbreak Linked to Jalapeño Peppers at Chipotle and QDOBA

www.scientificamerican.com · Thu, 06 Aug 2026 14:00:00 +0000

The CDC has issued a public health warning regarding a Salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeño peppers served at Chipotle and QDOBA restaurant chains.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has issued a public health warning regarding a Salmonella outbreak linked to fresh jalapeño peppers supplied to popular restaurant chains, including Chipotle Mexican Grill and QDOBA Mexican Eats. Federal officials, alongside state health departments, are actively investigating the distribution networks to determine the origin of the contaminated produce and limit further exposure among consumers across impacted regions.

This latest health alert arrives at a time when public health agencies are already tracking other foodborne illnesses affecting the national food pipeline. Several individuals have reported falling ill with symptoms typical of Salmonella infection after consuming food prepared with jalapeños at affected dining locations. Common symptoms include fever, stomach cramps, and severe diarrhea, which can become particularly severe for young children, the elderly, and individuals with weakened immune systems. Both restaurant chains are fully cooperating with health investigators and taking proactive steps to remove suspect produce from their kitchens.

Supply chain investigators are focusing their efforts on identifying the specific farms, packaging facilities, or distributors responsible for shipping contaminated peppers into the food service market. Health authorities continue to emphasize the vital importance of strict sanitation standards and supply chain transparency to protect public health. The CDC advises anyone experiencing severe symptoms after dining at affected establishments to seek prompt medical attention while the active investigation continues to identify all potential source points.
