From Creators to Babysitters: How AI Is Reshaping Work and Hiring at Anthropic

www.fastcompany.com · 2026-10-19T21:00:00

An internal Anthropic report reveals that widespread AI adoption has transformed employee roles into managing autonomous agents, increased meeting hours, and drastically reduced junior hiring.

Artificial intelligence startup Anthropic has released an internal report detailing how its workplace dynamics have shifted following the heavy adoption of its own Claude tools. While the company credits AI with driving substantial productivity gains in coding, sales, and contract processing, human involvement has increasingly emerged as an operational bottleneck. AI agents at Anthropic now log six times more hours than their human counterparts, yet spend roughly 80 percent of their session time waiting for human guidance rather than actively executing tasks.

As a result, many employees report a fundamental transformation in their daily responsibilities. According to an internal survey, about 20 percent of staff noted their roles have shifted from directly doing work to managing or "babysitting" Claude agents. Workers likened the experience to supervising children who treat minor requests and critical emergencies with equal urgency. Furthermore, the reliance on automated systems has altered collaboration patterns, with median employees spending 20 percent more time in meetings compared to the previous year as human coordination remains indispensable.

The aggressive delegation of tasks to AI agents has also reshaped Anthropic's workforce structure, pointing toward potential broader labor market trends. With Claude taking over routine tasks such as basic coding and bug detection, the demand for entry-level talent has plummeted. The proportion of junior hires in technical departments dropped sharply from 27 percent to just 9 percent since early 2025, suggesting that future workplaces heavily powered by AI may increasingly prioritize experienced professionals over junior personnel.
