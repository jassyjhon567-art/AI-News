xAI to Remove Unpermitted Mississippi Data Center Gas Turbines Over One-Year Period

www.engadget.com · Fri, 31 Jul 2026 20:49:45 +0000

xAI announced a one-year plan to remove unpermitted gas turbines from its Mississippi data center following environmental complaints and Clean Air Act violation claims from the NAACP.

Artificial intelligence technology company xAI has announced that it will take up to a full year to completely decommission and remove several unpermitted natural gas turbines operating at its high-performance data center in Mississippi. The decision comes after intense regulatory scrutiny and public outcry led by environmental advocates and the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP), which filed legal complaints alleging that the company violated the federal Clean Air Act by operating high-emission power generation equipment without proper regulatory authorization.

The controversy involves numerous temporary industrial gas turbines deployed at the facility to rapidly supply the massive electrical power required for training advanced artificial intelligence models. Environmental groups discovered that these energy-intensive turbines were brought online without securing necessary air quality permits from state regulators or conducting required environmental impact assessments. Local residents and civil rights organizations expressed deep concerns regarding public health hazards, highlighting elevated emissions of smog-forming nitrogen oxides and other toxic pollutants in surrounding residential communities.

Addressing the environmental and regulatory challenges, the company explained that a gradual, phased removal process is necessary to transition the facility toward permanent regional electrical grid connections without causing severe operational disruptions to its massive supercomputing infrastructure. Over the coming twelve months, technical teams will systematically decommission the unauthorized power units while alternative utility connections and fully compliant energy solutions are integrated into the site. State environmental authorities and civil rights organizations have emphasized that they will strictly monitor the year-long removal process to ensure full compliance with public health standards and environmental protection regulations.
