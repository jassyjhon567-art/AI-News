Biomemory Debuts DNA Shield as Its First Commercial Molecular Storage Product

www.darkreading.com · Fri, 16 Oct 2026 19:22:12 GMT

Biomemory has launched DNA Shield, its first commercial molecular IT product, following its acquisition of Catalog's assets and the appointment of CEO Laurent Guengant.

Biomemory has officially launched DNA Shield, marking the company's first commercial product built upon its proprietary molecular information technology platform. This market debut represents a major step forward in the practical adoption of molecular data storage, demonstrating that biological mechanisms can be reliably adapted to solve modern technological challenges. Engineered to counter the growing vulnerabilities and physical limitations of traditional data infrastructure, DNA Shield is designed to deliver secure, ultra-high-density, and stable data preservation for institutions seeking dependable, long-term archiving solutions across varied operational environments.

The commercial introduction closely follows several key corporate and strategic milestones achieved by Biomemory earlier this year. Most notably, the company significantly bolstered its research capabilities and intellectual property foundation through the acquisition of assets from Catalog, an established pioneer in the DNA computing and storage sector. In conjunction with this strategic acquisition, the company appointed Laurent Guengant as its new Chief Executive Officer, placing experienced leadership at the helm to guide commercial growth, oversee product delivery, and scale the molecular platform for broader industry adoption.

With the rollout of DNA Shield, Biomemory seeks to demonstrate the real-world viability of molecular IT for mission-critical archiving demands. As worldwide data production continues to expand exponentially, conventional magnetic tape and optical storage media face severe constraints regarding longevity, environmental footprint, and recurring maintenance costs. Molecular storage technologies address these bottlenecks by utilizing biological building blocks to encode digital information, offering an exceptionally compact, durable, and energy-conscious alternative that can safely preserve vital records across extended timeframes without requiring continuous electrical power or frequent hardware refreshes.
