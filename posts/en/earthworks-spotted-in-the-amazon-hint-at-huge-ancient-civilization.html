Massive Amazonian Earthworks Reveal Scale of Ancient Aquiry Civilization

www.nature.com · 2026-07-29

The discovery of nearly 400 ancient Amazonian earthworks is redefining the scale of the Aquiry civilization, alongside groundbreaking fossil evidence of the earliest known squid ancestor.

Archaeological investigations across the Amazon basin have uncovered nearly 400 ancient earthworks, offering groundbreaking insights into the extent of prehistoric human habitation within the region. These newly identified geometric structures, long concealed beneath dense rainforest canopy, strongly suggest that the Amazon was once home to a complex and far more expansive society than previously believed. Researchers emphasize that these extensive physical remains could drastically redefine current scientific understanding regarding the true spatial scale, organizational structure, and population density of the ancient Aquiry civilization.

The discovery of these large-scale enclosures, ditches, and raised mounds highlights the sophisticated landscape engineering capabilities possessed by early indigenous populations. Rather than living in a pristine and undisturbed wilderness, early inhabitants actively modified vast stretches of the Amazonian landscape to facilitate agricultural practices, complex ceremonial gatherings, and organized social structures. As modern aerial mapping and remote sensing technologies continue to survey previously unexplored regions, experts anticipate uncovering even more evidence of interconnected settlements and trade networks throughout the vast river basin.

Alongside these remarkable Amazonian archaeological findings, researchers have simultaneously reported the discovery of tiny fossils representing the earliest known direct ancestor of modern squids. These exceptionally preserved microscopic specimens provide crucial evolutionary insights into marine life during ancient geological epochs, helping paleontologists trace the biological roots of modern cephalopods. Taken together, these dual scientific breakthroughs publish crucial evidence that continues to reshape our overall understanding of both early human societal developments and the deep evolutionary history of life on Earth.
