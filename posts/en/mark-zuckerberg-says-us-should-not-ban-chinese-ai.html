Mark Zuckerberg Warns Against US Ban on Chinese AI, Citing Risks to Innovation

www.ft.com · Wed, 29 Jul 2026 02:15:21 GMT

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg has voiced opposition to banning Chinese artificial intelligence in the US, warning that excessive regulation and protectionism could harm domestic innovation and concentrate market power.

Mark Zuckerberg, Chief Executive Officer of Meta, has publicly voiced opposition to proposals calling for a ban on Chinese artificial intelligence technology in the United States. Speaking on the evolving landscape of global technology policy, the Meta chief argued that outright prohibitions on foreign AI systems could ultimately backfire, hindering American innovation and limiting the growth of the broader technology ecosystem. Instead of imposing blanket restrictions, he emphasized that maintaining open market competition and strengthening domestic technological capabilities represents a far more effective strategy for preserving global leadership in artificial intelligence.

Furthermore, Zuckerberg cautioned lawmakers against the dangers of regulatory capture, a scenario where entrenched corporate interests manipulate government oversight to protect their market dominance and stifle emerging competitors. He warned that excessively restrictive policies, even those introduced under the umbrella of national security, could inadvertently consolidate control within a small group of dominant American tech firms. Such concentration of power, he noted, threatens to restrict open-source developments and weaken the vibrant community of independent developers driving groundbreaking technological advancements.

As geopolitical tensions surrounding artificial intelligence continue to intensify between Washington and Beijing, policymakers face growing pressure to formulate comprehensive regulatory frameworks. Zuckerberg’s remarks highlight a fundamental rift within the technology sector regarding the future of open-source artificial intelligence versus proprietary models. Meta has consistently advocated for an open approach, arguing that transparent, accessible frameworks allow Western developers to establish global benchmarks, foster rapid innovation, and effectively counterbalance foreign technological influence without resorting to protectionist measures.
