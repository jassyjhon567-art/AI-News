Micron Urges Trump Administration to Block Apple From Using Chinese Memory Chips

9to5mac.com · Sat, 25 Jul 2026 02:03:00 +0000

Micron is lobbying the Trump administration to block Apple from purchasing Chinese memory chips, citing national security concerns and unfair competition for domestic semiconductor manufacturers.

American semiconductor manufacturer Micron Technology is actively lobbying the Trump administration to prevent tech giant Apple from sourcing memory chips from Chinese suppliers, according to a recent report by The Wall Street Journal. Micron has expressed grave concerns that Apple’s potential partnership with Chinese chipmakers could severely undermine U.S. national security interests while placing domestic semiconductor producers at a significant commercial disadvantage. This high-profile dispute highlights the escalating friction among major American technology corporations as geopolitical trade tensions between Washington and Beijing continue to intensify.

Apple has reportedly been exploring strategic supply deals with Chinese memory chip manufacturers to diversify its global supply chain and lower production costs for its flagship devices. However, Micron argues that allowing a dominant U.S. corporation to rely on heavily subsidized, state-backed Chinese suppliers poses critical economic and strategic risks to the domestic market. Micron executives have raised these concerns directly with senior federal officials, urging the administration to enforce stricter policy measures, tariffs, or trade restrictions to curb Beijing's growing influence in the global semiconductor sector.

The intense lobbying effort positions the Trump administration at a critical juncture regarding international technology trade policy and supply chain security. While Apple aims to preserve global operational efficiency and competitive device pricing, domestic semiconductor manufacturers maintain that decisive government intervention is essential to protect American technological leadership. The ultimate resolution of this clash could set a monumental precedent for how future U.S. administrations regulate supply chain dependencies on foreign components suppliers during an era of heightened geopolitical rivalry.
