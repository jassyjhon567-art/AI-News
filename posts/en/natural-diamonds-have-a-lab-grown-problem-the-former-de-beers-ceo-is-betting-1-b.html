Former De Beers CEO Bets $1 Billion to Revive Natural Diamond Demand Against Lab-Grown Surge

www.inc.com · Thu, 30 Jul 2026 08:00:00 -0400

Former De Beers CEO Gareth Penny is leading a $1 billion consortium effort to revive natural diamond demand through strategic marketing against rising lab-grown competition.

Gareth Penny, the former Chief Executive Officer of De Beers who successfully guided the diamond giant through past major financial crises, is returning to the spotlight with an ambitious recovery plan. Leading a prominent international consortium, Penny is spearheading a massive $1 billion initiative aimed at revitalizing the global natural diamond industry. This unprecedented venture comes at a critical juncture as traditional mined gems face significant market disruptions, driven by changing consumer behaviors and intense competitive pressures from synthetic alternatives.

In recent years, the rapid emergence of lab-grown diamonds has fundamentally altered the global luxury landscape. Offering stones that are chemically and visually identical to mined gems at a fraction of the cost, lab-grown alternatives have captured a substantial share of the consumer market, particularly among younger demographic groups. This rapid market shift has severely eroded profit margins for traditional mining operations, depressed wholesale prices across the global supply chain, and challenged the historical status of mined diamonds as rare luxury investments.

To counter this growing threat, Penny’s consortium believes that strategic marketing and clear brand differentiation hold the key to restoring consumer confidence in natural stones. The planned investment will fund comprehensive global promotional campaigns designed to highlight the unique geological history, enduring emotional significance, and long-term value retention of mined diamonds. By emphasizing the distinct authenticity and emotional power of natural creation compared to factory-manufactured options, the consortium aims to reignite consumer desire and secure a sustainable future for mined gems.
