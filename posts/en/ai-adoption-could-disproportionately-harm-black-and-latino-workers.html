AI Expansion Threatens to Disproportionately Displace Black and Latino Workers, Report Warns

www.fastcompany.com · 2026-11-20T22:30:00

A report warns that AI-driven automation could disproportionately displace Black and Latino workers in lower-wage roles, threatening economic mobility unless proactive reskilling policies are implemented.

While public conversation around artificial intelligence often centers on disruptions to white-collar professions, emerging data indicates that non-college-educated, low-wage workers will bear the brunt of automation. A report from the McKinsey Institute for Economic Mobility highlights that Black and Latino workers are particularly vulnerable, as they are overrepresented in sectors where AI and robotics will eliminate jobs entirely rather than augment existing tasks. Projections reveal that roughly 40 percent of work hours carried out by Black employees and 41 percent by Latino workers face displacement, compared to 37 percent for white workers.

This disparity threatens to sever critical pathways to economic advancement. Historically, roles in customer service, postal delivery, manual packing, and physical labor have served as crucial stepping stones to the middle class for marginalized communities. However, these specific occupations face some of the steepest declines in labor demand due to AI-driven automation. Overall, lower-wage workers are over seven times more likely to require career transitions than higher earners, while those without college degrees are nearly twice as likely to need retraining.

Conversely, white workers are substantially better positioned to benefit from the ongoing technological transformation. They are more prevalent in sectors attracting heavy private capital—such as semiconductors, robotics, and biotechnology—where AI boosts productivity and generates wealth rather than eliminating roles. Without targeted workforce reskilling programs and proactive policy interventions, the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence risks deepening existing racial and socioeconomic inequalities across the labor market.
