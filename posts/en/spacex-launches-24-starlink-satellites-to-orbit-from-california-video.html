SpaceX Successfully Launches 24 Starlink Satellites from California Coast

www.space.com · Sat, 08 Aug 2026 19:19:16 +0000

SpaceX has launched 24 Starlink satellites into low Earth orbit from California, successfully landing the Falcon 9 first-stage booster on a Pacific droneship.

Aerospace company SpaceX has successfully launched another batch of 24 Starlink internet-beaming satellites into orbit from California’s scenic central coast. The impressive lift-off took place from Vandenberg Space Force Base, utilizing a workhorse Falcon 9 rocket to transport the payload into low Earth orbit. Spectators along the coast enjoyed clear views of the rocket as it ascended into the sky. This latest mission represents another step forward in the company's ongoing effort to expand its massive orbital network and bolster global telecommunications infrastructure.

Following a smooth stage separation shortly after launch, the Falcon 9’s first-stage booster executed a precise controlled descent back through the atmosphere. The reusable booster landed safely on an autonomous SpaceX droneship stationed in the Pacific Ocean off the California coast. Engineers on the ground confirmed that all launch systems functioned within normal parameters during the flight. This successful landing further demonstrates the company's commitment to rocket reusability, a key factor that continues to drastically lower the financial costs of spaceflight while enabling a rapid launch cadence.

Meanwhile, the rocket's upper stage successfully deployed the 24 Starlink satellites into their intended low Earth orbits. Over the coming weeks, these satellites will use their onboard ion thrusters to maneuver into their final operational positions. Once fully integrated into the vast Starlink constellation, which already features thousands of active satellites, they will help provide reliable, high-speed internet access to users worldwide, particularly those living in remote or traditionally underserved regions. SpaceX plans to maintain its aggressive launch schedule throughout the remainder of the year to meet growing global demand.
