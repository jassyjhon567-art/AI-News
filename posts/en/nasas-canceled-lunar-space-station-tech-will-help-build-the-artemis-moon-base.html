Repurposed Space Station Technology to Support NASA's Artemis Lunar Base

www.space.com · Thu, 13 Aug 2026 19:00:00 +0000

Northrop Grumman is adapting technology from the HALO module, originally intended for a lunar space station, to support NASA's upcoming Artemis moon base.

Aerospace manufacturer Northrop Grumman is planning to repurpose advanced technology initially developed for NASA's lunar space station module to support the construction of the Artemis moon base. The initiative aims to transition hardware and engineering solutions from the Habitation and Logistics Outpost, commonly known as HALO, into viable surface architecture for long-term lunar exploration.

The HALO module was originally designed to serve as an initial living and operational hub for astronauts aboard the planned lunar orbital platform. Built with sophisticated environmental control, life support systems, power distribution capabilities, and advanced communication networks, the underlying technology remains highly relevant for planetary environments. By adapting these mature systems for ground operations, engineers can address the harsh environmental challenges of the lunar terrain more efficiently.

This strategic pivot is expected to save significant time and financial resources for NASA's broader Artemis campaign. Reusing existing designs and certified components reduces the need to develop entirely new surface habitation systems from scratch. As international space agencies and commercial partners work toward establishing a sustainable human presence on the Moon, integrating proven orbital technologies into surface infrastructure marks a pragmatic step forward in deep-space exploration.
