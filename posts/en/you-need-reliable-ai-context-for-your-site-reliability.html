How Context Engineering and AI Are Transforming Modern Site Reliability

stackoverflow.blog · Tue, 28 Jul 2026 07:40:00 GMT

Asaf Savich of Komodor discusses how context engineering enables AI tools to streamline site reliability engineering, shifting human roles toward AI management and high-level strategy.

In a recent industry discussion, host Ryan sat down with Asaf Savich, the AI Engineering Group Manager at Komodor, to explore the changing landscape of site reliability engineering (SRE). As modern cloud architectures become increasingly distributed and microservice-driven, operational reliability now demands navigating immense amounts of cross-service context. Engineering teams are constantly tasked with understanding intricate service dependencies, tracing cascading failures, and maintaining visibility across complex multi-cloud environments, making traditional troubleshooting methods far less effective.

To overcome these operational challenges, the integration of artificial intelligence into reliability workflows has gained significant traction, but its success hinges on robust context engineering. Savich explained that AI agents require rich, well-structured, and highly relevant contextual data from across logs, metrics, traces, and system topologies to deliver reliable insights. Without precise context engineering, AI models risk delivering false positives or hallucinated solutions. Providing AI with accurate context enables automated systems to correctly pinpoint root causes, speed up incident response, and reduce mean time to resolution.

As a result of these technological advancements, the responsibilities of human SREs are undergoing a major transition from manual operational tasks to strategic oversight and AI agent management. Rather than spending countless hours manually sifting through logs or executing routine diagnostic steps, engineers are moving into roles where they curate context, supervise intelligent automation tools, and design resilient operational guardrails. Ultimately, this shift allows engineering teams to focus on long-term infrastructure resilience, strategic system architecture, and overall business growth while AI handles routine troubleshooting.
