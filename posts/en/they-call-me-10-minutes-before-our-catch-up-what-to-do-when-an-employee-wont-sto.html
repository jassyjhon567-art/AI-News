How to Handle an Employee Who Calls Too Often Without Ruining Your Working Relationship

www.inc.com · Mon, 10 Aug 2026 08:30:00 -0400

Management expert Alison Green shares effective strategies for guiding over-communicating employees toward digital messaging tools while preserving positive professional relationships and workplace productivity.

Effective workplace communication is essential for team success, but excessive phone calls from enthusiastic or anxious team members can severely disrupt productivity and workflow. Managers frequently find themselves struggling to balance open-door accessibility with the need for uninterrupted deep focus time. When direct reports resort to spontaneous phone calls for non-urgent matters—or reach out mere minutes before a scheduled catch-up—it creates unnecessary friction and fragments the workday. Management expert Alison Green addresses this common workplace dilemma, offering actionable strategies to establish healthier communication boundaries.

To resolve this issue without dampening morale or alienating staff, managers should set explicit guidelines regarding appropriate communication channels. Steering team members toward asynchronous collaboration platforms such as Slack, Microsoft Teams, or standard email allows routine queries to be handled efficiently without causing immediate operational disruption. Leaders can gently explain that unscheduled calls break critical concentration, encouraging employees to batch non-urgent questions for regular catch-ups or written messages instead. Framing this operational shift around mutual productivity ensures staff understand it is a professional boundary, not a personal rejection.

Sustaining a healthy, collaborative working relationship requires empathetic messaging and consistent policy enforcement. If an employee repeatedly breaks these norms, a direct yet supportive conversation becomes essential. Managers need to clarify communication hierarchies by clearly defining what constitutes a genuine operational emergency that warrants a phone call versus what belongs in a chat thread. By establishing clear expectations and demonstrating patience throughout the adjustment period, leaders can safeguard their schedules while making sure team members feel fully supported, informed, and valued.
