Retaining Physician-Scientists in Academia Is Vital for the Future of Health Research

www.nature.com · 2026-10-12

Greater funding stability and dedicated career support are urgently needed to prevent medical doctors from leaving academic health research.

Physician-scientists play an indispensable role in modern medicine, effectively bridging the gap between clinical bedside practice and innovative laboratory research. However, retaining medical doctors within academic institutions has developed into a critical challenge for global health systems. As pressures mount across clinical environments and universities alike, an increasing number of trained physicians are stepping away from academic research roles in pursuit of the financial rewards and relative stability of full-time medical practice or private industry positions.

The primary drivers behind this continuous exodus stem from persistent structural hurdles, notably unstable grant funding mechanisms and intense clinical workloads. Clinician-investigators face the unique and demanding responsibility of managing patient care while simultaneously conducting rigorous, time-consuming scientific investigations. Unlike colleagues who focus solely on research or clinical work, these dual-role professionals frequently encounter precarious short-term contracts, insufficient mentorship, and a lack of institutional recognition for their specialized cross-disciplinary expertise.

Without urgent intervention, health-care research risks losing the essential clinical perspectives that drive translational breakthroughs and directly improve patient outcomes. Academic institutions, university leadership, and public funding bodies must urgently collaborate to establish more supportive career frameworks. Offering dedicated protected research time, sustainable long-term funding models, and clearer career progression will be essential to retaining this invaluable group of scientists and safeguarding the future of translational medical discoveries worldwide.
