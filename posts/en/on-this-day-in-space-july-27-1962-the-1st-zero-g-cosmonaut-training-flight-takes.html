On This Day in Space: Soviet Cosmonauts Experience First Zero-G Training Flight in 1962

www.space.com · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 17:20:20 +0000

On July 27, 1962, Soviet cosmonauts completed the first zero-gravity training flight aboard a jetliner, pioneering atmospheric microgravity simulation for future human space exploration.

On July 27, 1962, the Soviet space program achieved a significant milestone in astronaut training by conducting the first zero-gravity flight aboard a specially adapted Soviet jetliner. On this historic day, two Russian cosmonauts boarded the aircraft to experience the unique physical phenomenon of weightlessness without ever leaving Earth's atmosphere. This groundbreaking approach allowed space agencies to study the physical and psychological effects of microgravity on human subjects in a controlled, reproducible environment before sending them on complex orbital missions during the height of the Space Race.

During the flight, the aircraft executed precise parabolic flight paths, diving and climbing rapidly to create brief intervals of free-fall. These short periods of weightlessness provided the cosmonauts with critical hands-on exposure to the disorienting sensations of zero gravity. Inside the cabin, the crew practiced basic operational procedures, tested floating equipment, and adapted to moving around without the pull of gravity. This practical experience was vital for preparing future spacefarers for the rigorous demands of long-duration spaceflight.

The successful training mission established parabolic atmospheric flights as an indispensable protocol for human spaceflight preparation. By pioneering this cost-effective and relatively safe method of microgravity simulation, the Soviet Union fundamentally transformed how spacefaring nations prepare their personnel. Today, zero-gravity aircraft flights remain a standard training tool utilized by space agencies around the globe, ensuring that modern astronauts are well-equipped to handle the challenging environments encountered during space exploration beyond Earth.
