Flight disruptions and cancellations loom as Icelandair faces shutdown over mechanics strike

Iceland’s national airline carrier, Icelandair, reportedly rejected an offer from aircraft mechanics on Tuesday, paving the way for a potential indefinite strike—starting on Wednesday, September 23—if the two parties don’t reach an agreement by midnight local time on Tuesday, or 8:00 p.m. EDT. Iceland serves as a major gateway for flights between the U.S.and Europe—as well as other parts of North America—and relies heavily on tourism. A strike would serve as a heavy blow not only to Icelandair, but also, the nation’s economy. For example, if the strike goes ahead, the dispute would halt all of Icelandair’s international and domestic flights, which account for some 63% of all passengers traveling through Iceland, according to the air carrier’s 2025 annual report , per Bloomberg. Fast Company has reached out to Icelandair for comment. It could also cause “considerable [disruption] right away” to business as usual on the island nation, which depends heavily on the transportation of goods—such as food staples like fresh fish—to and from the country, according to Islandsbanki hf chief economist Jón Bjarki Bentsson, who spoke with Bloomberg. So much so, that Iceland’s parliament has previously intervened in critical worker disputes and previously banned mechanics for Icelandair and the coast guard from striking. What is the potential strike about? At issue is a dispute over pay. Members of the Icelandic Aircraft Mechanics Association (FVFÍ) say Icelandair rejected their offer to postpone the strike by a week, and that they have made repeated attempts to return to the negotiating table. “It is disappointing to find ourselves wanting to keep the dialogue going with the aim of finding a solution to the dispute, only to receive no response,” FVFÍ said in a statement. “When one of the negotiating parties is prepared to do its part to seek a path towards an agreement, give the talks more time and work with a proposal from the State Mediator, but that opportunity is not accepted, it is reasonable to question whether there is a genuine willingness to reach an agreement.”
