Prehistoric Origins: Seals Evolved Amphibious Hearing Millions of Years Ago

www.sciencenews.org · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 13:00:00 +0000

A new study analyzing high-resolution scans of marine mammal ears reveals that seals evolved amphibious hearing millions of years ago during their early transition to aquatic life.

A groundbreaking evolutionary study has revealed that the remarkable ability of seals and other pinnipeds to hear effectively both underwater and on land originated far earlier in their evolutionary history than previously thought. Pinnipeds, a group of semi-aquatic marine mammals that includes seals, sea lions, and walruses, rely heavily on their sense of hearing for navigating, hunting, and communicating across dual environments. By analyzing high-resolution anatomical scans of various marine mammal ear structures, researchers have traced the origins of this unique amphibious auditory adaptation back millions of years.

To uncover the timeline of this evolutionary milestone, an international team of scientists conducted high-resolution CT scans on the ear bones of hundreds of living and extinct marine mammals. By examining the structural density, geometry, and middle-ear mechanics, the researchers were able to model how sound waves travel through these specialized ears in different mediums. The comparative analysis demonstrated that key physical adaptations required for dual-medium hearing emerged during the early transition of pinniped ancestors from land-dwelling carnivorans into semi-aquatic ocean foragers.

These findings significantly alter our understanding of sensory evolution in marine mammals, demonstrating that acute underwater hearing did not require a complete sacrifice of aerial auditory capabilities. Instead, early pinnipeds developed a versatile auditory system capable of processing sound efficiently in air and water almost simultaneously. Understanding how these ancient creatures adapted their sensory systems provides valuable insights into how modern seals navigate complex coastal ecosystems and highlights the evolutionary pressures that shaped marine mammal diversity over millions of years.
