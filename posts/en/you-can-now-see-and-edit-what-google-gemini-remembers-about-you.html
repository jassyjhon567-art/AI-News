Google Gemini Now Lets Users View and Edit Stored Memories

www.engadget.com · Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 +0000

Google Gemini now allows users to view, edit, and delete stored personal memories to better control how the AI assistant personalizes its responses.

Google has officially rolled out a new feature for its AI assistant, Gemini, allowing users to view, manage, and edit the personal information and preferences the model retains over time. As artificial intelligence tools become more deeply embedded in daily productivity and personal tasks, contextual memory has emerged as a crucial component for delivering seamless assistance. With this latest update, individuals can now exercise direct oversight and enjoy enhanced transparency regarding what Gemini remembers across past conversations.

Through the platform's memory management settings, users can inspect a clear list of stored details, ranging from professional roles and dietary choices to preferred writing styles and personal interests. If any recorded information is inaccurate or becomes outdated, it can be quickly updated or removed entirely. Furthermore, users can manually specify facts they want the AI to remember, allowing them to proactively shape how Gemini responds to inquiries without needing to repeat context in every new prompt.

This development reflects a broader movement across the technology sector to balance AI-driven personalization with robust user privacy and data security. While retaining information significantly improves the relevance and efficiency of an assistant, long-term data storage has raised valid privacy concerns among consumers. By providing simple controls to audit and alter stored memories, Google aims to give users reliable tools to govern their personal data as intelligent systems continue to evolve.
