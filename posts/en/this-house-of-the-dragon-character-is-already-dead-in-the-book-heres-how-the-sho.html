How 'House of the Dragon' Altered Gwayne Hightower's Story from the Books

www.businessinsider.com · 2026-07-27T01:27:01Z

'House of the Dragon' expands Gwayne Hightower's role by keeping him alive, offering a fresh perspective on the Hightower family compared to George R.R. Martin's original book.

In the second season of "House of the Dragon," viewers are introduced in greater detail to Ser Gwayne Hightower, the stubborn yet capable brother of Dowager Queen Alicent and son of the former Hand, Otto Hightower. Arriving from Oldtown with military reinforcements, his presence brings a fresh dynamic to the Red Keep and the battlefield. His inclusion on screen allows audiences to explore the Hightower family's internal dynamics, noble pride, and complex motivations during the early stages of the bloody Targaryen civil war, known as the Dance of the Dragons.

This screen portrayal marks a significant departure from George R.R. Martin's source book, "Fire & Blood." In the original literary lore, Gwayne Hightower played a much smaller role and met his demise far earlier in the timeline. In the text, he was serving as an officer in the City Watch of King's Landing when the city fell, leading to his swift death. Instead of adhering to this brief fate, the show creators chose to keep him alive, turning him into a key companion traveling alongside Lord Commander Ser Criston Cole through the Crownlands.

By altering his tragic fate and expanding his story, the showrunners effectively humanize the Green faction and offer a grounded perspective on the devastating impact of dragon warfare. Gwayne's cynical banter and growing horror at the reality of combat give the audience a surrogate through which to experience the terror of dragons. This deliberate creative change adds emotional depth and dramatic tension to the television narrative, enriching the lore beyond its printed origins.
