Beyond Straws and Squeezable Tubes: How Microgravity Reshapes How Astronauts Eat and Drink

www.scientificamerican.com · Sun, 09 Aug 2026 10:00:00 +0000

Scientists are studying how microgravity alters human physiology and liquid dynamics to develop better nutrition systems for long-duration space missions.

As space agencies and private commercial enterprises prepare for extended long-duration missions to the Moon, Mars, and deeper into the solar system, scientists are turning their attention to a fundamental aspect of human survival: how astronauts consume food and liquids in microgravity. Living in zero gravity significantly alters human physiological processes, making simple daily routines like chewing and swallowing surprisingly complex compared to life on Earth. In space, body fluids shift upward toward the head, causing nasal congestion similar to a cold, which drastically dampens the sense of taste and smell and often leads to a diminished appetite among crew members.

Beyond the internal biological changes, the physical behavior of food and fluids in orbit creates significant engineering and operational obstacles. In a weightless environment, liquids do not pour; instead, they coalesce into floating spheres that must be consumed through sealed pouches or specialized straws to prevent stray droplets from damaging sensitive electronics. Furthermore, standard dry foods that produce crumbs present severe hazards, as drifting particles can be accidentally inhaled by astronauts or clog critical air filtration systems. Consequently, food scientists and aerospace engineers are collaborating to design innovative packaging, advanced eating utensils, and appetizing menus that satisfy both physiological and psychological needs.

Addressing these complex challenges is vital for ensuring the physical and mental well-being of crews on future long-distance space voyages. Prolonged periods of poor appetite or insufficient calorie intake can result in rapid muscle atrophy, significant loss of bone density, and impaired cognitive function, all of which compromise mission safety. By conducting thorough studies on how space travel alters metabolism, sensory perception, and digestion, researchers aim to develop customized nutritional protocols that keep astronauts fully nourished, healthy, and psychologically grounded as humanity expands its footprint across the cosmos.
