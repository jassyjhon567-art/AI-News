Historic 1915 Home's Flawed Closet Bathroom Transformed into a "Pink Paradise"

www.apartmenttherapy.com · Sat, 07 Nov 2026 15:45:00 -0500

A couple revitalized an awkwardly placed guest bathroom in their historic 1915 home by repairing decades of water damage and incorporating original tiles to create a "Pink Paradise."

When Laura Trexler and her husband purchased their 1915 historic residence, they inherited a home filled with vintage charm but burdened by several decades of unconventional modifications. During the 1980s, previous owners had converted the property into a commercial bed and breakfast, introducing several awkward layouts to accommodate guests. Among the most unusual structural decisions was the second-floor guest bathroom, which had been built directly inside an existing bedroom closet, resulting in an impractical and cramped setup that clearly failed to meet modern living standards.

Faced with compounding structural issues, the couple decided to embark on a massive, whole-house overhaul rather than attempting piecemeal repairs. This ambitious undertaking required extensive rewiring and a complete overhaul of the plumbing infrastructure across nearly every room. The guest bathroom quickly emerged as a critical concern requiring immediate structural intervention. Decades of neglected moisture issues had taken a severe toll, as a persistent leak had completely rotted the subfloor beneath the fixtures, while a deteriorating vanity sink continued to cause water damage, threatening the integrity of the room.

Despite the extensive structural destruction, the homeowners were determined to preserve the historic integrity of the dwelling. By carefully salvaging, cleaning, and incorporating original tiles discovered within the home, Trexler transformed the once-troubled space into a vibrant and whimsical "Pink Paradise." The thoughtful redesign not only solved deep-seated mechanical and plumbing hazards but also elevated a previously awkward closet conversion into an elegant, functional centerpiece. The finished bathroom now stands as a striking testament to balancing heritage preservation with modern, creative interior design.
