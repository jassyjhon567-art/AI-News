Russian State Cyber Unit Linked to Exploitation of Public Wi-Fi Gateways

www.securityweek.com · Mon, 03 Aug 2026 09:17:37 +0000

Russian state-sponsored threat group Midnight Blizzard is targeting public Wi-Fi networks at hospitality venues to steal Microsoft account credentials and breach corporate targets.

Cybersecurity experts have linked a recent wave of cyberattacks targeting public Wi-Fi gateways at various hospitality organizations to Midnight Blizzard, a prominent state-sponsored advanced persistent threat group associated with Russian intelligence. The malicious campaign primarily aims to intercept network traffic and compromise guest and enterprise connections to gain unauthorized access to sensitive operational networks.

According to researchers, the threat actors deploy sophisticated techniques to gain initial access to Wi-Fi management systems at hotels and venue centers. Once inside the network infrastructure, the attackers conduct man-in-the-middle operations designed to harvest Microsoft account credentials from unsuspecting guests and employees. This allows the group to bypass traditional perimeter defenses and establish persistent access to high-value corporate targets using stolen authentication tokens.

The targeting of public Wi-Fi infrastructure highlights an evolving strategy among state-backed hackers to exploit weaker peripheral networks as stepping stones toward broader espionage goals. Security agencies recommend that hospitality venues immediately audit their Wi-Fi gateway hardware, apply necessary firmware patches, and enforce multi-factor authentication. Meanwhile, travelers and remote workers are strongly advised to utilize virtual private networks when connecting to public networks.
