Researchers Capture Rare Airborne Gliding of Neon Flying Squid in the Pacific

www.schneier.com · 2026-08-21T21:07:20Z

Scientists have documented a shoal of neon flying squid launching from the Pacific Ocean and gliding in synchronized formation over tens of meters.

A remarkable sighting in the Pacific Ocean roughly 370 miles off the coast of Tokyo has provided concrete photographic evidence of cephalopods taking flight. Researchers aboard a vessel witnessed an unexpected spectacle when a shoal of around 100 squid erupted from the surface of the water, propelling themselves into the air and gliding in near-unison alongside the boat for a distance of approximately 30 meters.

Identified by scientists as likely being neon flying squid (Ommastrephes bartramii), these creatures belong to a specialized family of roughly 20 squid species known to leap from the water. While marine biologists were aware of their leaping capabilities, the ability of these animals to maintain aerodynamic gliding above the surface had long remained unverified and largely the subject of maritime lore until these historic photographs were secured.

The synchronized movement of the shoal offered valuable insights into cephalopod locomotion and escape behavior. Experts suggest that such airborne maneuvers may serve as an energy-efficient mechanism to evade underwater predators or facilitate rapid travel across the open ocean. This unprecedented photographic documentation confirms that certain squid species possess sophisticated aerodynamic adaptations, broadening scientific understanding of pelagic marine life.
