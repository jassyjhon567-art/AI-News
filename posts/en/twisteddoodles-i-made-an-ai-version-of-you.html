Twisteddoodles Explores the Absurdity of Personal Artificial Intelligence Clones

www.newscientist.com · Wed, 07 Oct 2026 17:00:00 +0000

The latest satirical cartoon by Twisteddoodles humorously examines the awkward social dynamics and ethical dilemmas surrounding the creation of personal artificial intelligence replicas.

The latest weekly installment from the popular cartoonist Twisteddoodles captures the evolving anxieties of the modern digital landscape through the lens of sharp, relatable humor. Featuring the premise of someone presenting a digital replica of an acquaintance with the phrase, "I made an AI version of you," the cartoon taps into a shared cultural unease. By framing cutting-edge technology within an awkward everyday interaction, the work highlights the bizarre realities that occur when generative tools intersect with ordinary human relationships.

This artistic commentary reflects a broader social phenomenon driven by the rapid rise of accessible generative artificial intelligence. As tools capable of mimicking voice patterns, visual likenesses, and conversational styles become widespread, the line between flattering tribute and intrusive surveillance continues to blur. Twisteddoodles illustrates that while technological capabilities have accelerated at a breakneck pace, the social etiquette and conversational boundaries governing their use remain largely undefined, leading to distinctly uncomfortable encounters.

Beyond simple comedy, the cartoon raises pressing questions regarding personal identity, digital consent, and the ethics of synthetic media. Casual replication of an individual's persona challenges conventional notions of privacy and autonomy in everyday life. Through its minimalist yet perceptive approach, Twisteddoodles successfully encourages viewers to consider where genuine human connection ends and simulated personality begins, offering a poignant critique of our increasingly automated world.
