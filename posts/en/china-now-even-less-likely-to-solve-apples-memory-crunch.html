Apple Unlikely to Turn to Chinese Chipmakers to Solve Ongoing Memory Shortage

9to5mac.com · Tue, 11 Aug 2026 11:01:05 +0000

Growing geopolitical tensions and political pressure from the US government have made it increasingly unlikely that Apple will source memory chips from Chinese suppliers CXMT and YMTC.

Apple's strategy to source memory chips from Chinese suppliers to mitigate global supply constraints is facing severe headwinds. The tech giant had previously considered procuring memory hardware, specifically RAM and flash storage, from prominent Chinese semiconductor firms ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) and Yangtze Memory Technologies Corp (YMTC). While such procurement was not explicitly illegal under international trade frameworks, it placed the Cupertino-based company directly in the crosshairs of geopolitical friction between Washington and Beijing.

Recent policy shifts and regulatory developments in the United States have further diminished the feasibility of this partnership. Broader national security concerns regarding Chinese technology infrastructure have prompted American lawmakers to intensify scrutiny on domestic tech companies engaging with foreign chipmakers. As a result, Apple faces escalating political pressure to avoid reliance on Chinese suppliers, making any potential contract with CXMT or YMTC a high-risk venture that could trigger federal sanctions or public backlash.

Consequently, Apple is being forced to re-evaluate its supply chain strategy and seek alternative solutions to address its memory component demands. While leaning on established suppliers in South Korea and Taiwan remains the safer operational route, it presents additional cost challenges and logistical bottlenecks. As global memory shortages persist, Apple must navigate complex geopolitical landscapes while securing sufficient hardware components to maintain its global device production schedules without compromising US regulatory compliance.
