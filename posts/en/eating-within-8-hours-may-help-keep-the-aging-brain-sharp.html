Time-Restricted Eating May Help Preserve Cognitive Skills in Older Adults

www.sciencedaily.com · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 05:02:45 EDT

A study shows that limiting daily eating to an eight-hour window may improve cognitive skills in older women, regardless of overall weight loss.

Restricting daily food consumption to a specific time window may offer significant benefits for cognitive health as people age, according to recent research findings. Researchers discovered that limiting eating to an eight-to-nine-hour window could help preserve key executive functions, such as planning, focus, and problem-solving abilities, in older individuals. This growing body of evidence suggests that the timing of daily meals might play a crucial role in maintaining brain function alongside traditional considerations like caloric intake and nutrition.

The clinical trial, conducted over a six-month period, focused on a group of older women who were assigned to different daily eating schedules. One group restricted their food intake to a targeted eight-to-nine-hour window, while the control group consumed their meals across a standard twelve-hour timeframe. Upon completing the study, participants in the time-restricted eating group demonstrated noticeably superior performance on cognitive assessments designed to evaluate mental flexibility, task switching, and complex planning skills compared to their peers.

Interestingly, both groups involved in the trial lost similar amounts of body weight over the course of six months. This critical finding indicates that the observed cognitive improvements were not merely a byproduct of overall weight reduction or lower calorie consumption. Instead, researchers hypothesize that the biological mechanisms triggered by longer daily fasting periods may directly support neural health, hinting that when people eat could be just as important as what or how much they eat.
