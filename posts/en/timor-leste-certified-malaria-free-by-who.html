WHO Certifies Timor-Leste as Malaria-Free After Historic Public Health Effort

www.who.int · Wed, 23 Jul 2025 10:07:41 Z

The World Health Organization has certified Timor-Leste as malaria-free, celebrating its transition from over 223,000 cases in 2006 to zero indigenous cases through sustained national interventions.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has officially certified Timor-Leste as malaria-free, marking a historic public health achievement for the young nation. Since gaining independence in 2002, the country prioritized eliminating the mosquito-borne disease, successfully curbing indigenous transmission nationwide. Timor-Leste is now the third country in the WHO South-East Asia region to achieve this status, following Maldives and Sri Lanka, and joins a global total of 47 countries and one territory certified free of the disease.

The nation underwent a dramatic transition, decreasing clinical cases from a peak of over 223,000 in 2006 down to zero indigenous cases from 2021 onwards. This progress began shortly after independence with the creation of the National Malaria Programme in 2003. Despite severe health worker shortages, Timor-Leste modernized its three-tier healthcare infrastructure, introduced artemisinin-based combination therapies, and deployed rapid diagnostic tests. Robust vector control measures—supported by partners like the Global Fund—such as indoor residual spraying and widespread distribution of insecticide-treated bed nets, played an instrumental role in interrupting transmission.

To maintain this status and prevent resurgence, health authorities established real-time case-based surveillance and community outreach clinics, ensuring equitable and free access to medical services even in remote rural areas. Border screening and ongoing local vigilance remain central to keeping malaria at bay. Health officials and global partners commended the collaborative efforts of local communities and dedicated frontline workers, emphasizing that sustained vigilance will be essential to protect future generations and safeguard this hard-won national milestone.
