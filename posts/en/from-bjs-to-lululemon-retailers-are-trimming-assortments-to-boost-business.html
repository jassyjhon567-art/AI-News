Retailers from BJ’s to Lululemon Trim Product Lines to Spur Business Growth

www.cnbc.com · Sat, 10 Oct 2026 12:00:01 GMT

Retailers including BJ’s and Lululemon are trimming product assortments to cut operational costs, improve inventory efficiency, and foster stable, long-term business growth.

Major retailers and specialized apparel brands, ranging from warehouse clubs like BJ's Wholesale Club to athletic wear giants like Lululemon, are actively scaling back their product lines. Faced with changing consumer spending patterns and ongoing economic pressures, companies are pivoting away from the long-standing strategy of offering an endless variety of choices. Instead, leadership teams are focusing on smaller, more targeted inventories designed to restore operational discipline, lower overhead, and return their businesses to healthy long-term growth.

For many big-box stores and specialty retailers, excessively wide product assortments have created notable operational hurdles, including crowded aisles, complicated supply chains, and costly inventory markdowns. By deliberately culling slower-selling items and refining their overall collections, merchants can emphasize their most popular, high-performing merchandise. This disciplined curation not only simplifies the browsing experience for shoppers but also enhances logistics management and allows store managers to optimize display space more effectively.

This industry-wide shift marks a clear transition from prioritizing top-line volume expansion to preserving profitability and stabilizing balance sheets. As retail leaders navigate shifting market conditions, a tighter assortment strategy helps protect brand equity by reducing the need for aggressive promotional discounts. Moving forward, the focus on essential, high-margin products over sheer quantity is likely to remain a central component of modern retail planning, helping organizations build more resilient commercial models.
