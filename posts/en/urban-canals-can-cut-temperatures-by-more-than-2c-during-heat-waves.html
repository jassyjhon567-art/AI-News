Urban Canals Can Lower Temperatures by Over 2°C During Heatwaves, Study Finds

phys.org · Mon, 10 Aug 2026 08:40:04 EDT

A study by The University of Manchester reveals that urban canals can cool immediate surrounding temperatures by over 2°C during heatwaves, offering crucial climate resilience benefits for cities.

A new study conducted by researchers at The University of Manchester reveals that urban canals can effectively cool their immediate surroundings by more than 2°C during intense heatwaves. As global climate change accelerates, extreme heat events are becoming increasingly frequent and severe across metropolitan areas worldwide. In response, urban centers are actively seeking sustainable blue-green infrastructure solutions to counteract the rising threat posed by the urban heat island effect.

The research underscores the fundamental mechanisms behind this localized cooling phenomenon. Water bodies naturally absorb environmental heat and lower the air temperature of adjacent spaces through the process of evaporation. According to the study, this microclimatic cooling effect provides measurable temperature reductions exceeding 2°C in the areas directly bordering canals. Consequently, this natural cooling benefit extends outward to nearby residential neighborhoods, commercial streets, and public walkways, delivering essential relief to local residents during extreme summer conditions.

These findings offer valuable insights for municipal authorities, landscape architects, and urban planners aiming to construct climate-resilient cities. Experts recommend prioritizing the restoration, maintenance, and integration of historic and modern canal networks into contemporary urban design frameworks. Combining these aquatic systems with surrounding green spaces, such as parks and tree-lined avenues, can dramatically enhance a city's thermal comfort, safeguard public health, and mitigate the broader impacts of global warming.
