Harnessing LLM-Generated Glossaries to Master Enterprise Terminology

martinfowler.com · 2026-11-12T09:15:00-05:00

Organizations are using LLMs to automatically generate context-specific glossaries from internal data, enhancing AI domain accuracy and preventing terminology confusion across specialized business operations.

When large organizations deploy large language models (LLMs) into their specialized operational domains, general-purpose models frequently struggle with internal jargon, company-specific acronyms, and unique terminology. Because these specialized expressions are largely absent from standard public training data, the operational effectiveness of AI systems significantly deteriorates. To address this persistent barrier, organizations can deploy an LLM to systematically analyze internal repositories—including technical documentation, project emails, meeting transcripts, and employee chat logs—to automatically extract, categorize, and define specialized terms and context-specific phrases.

To ensure precision, human subject-matter experts must review and refine these machine-generated drafts, correcting misinterpretations and clarifying ambiguous usages. Furthermore, rather than attempting to enforce a single, rigid corporate dictionary, modern software architecture recommends organizing glossaries around distinct "bounded contexts." Different business units, specialized operational teams, and software codebases frequently use identical words in fundamentally distinct ways. Maintaining separate, context-aware glossaries prevents operational confusion, reduces context token consumption for AI agents, and ensures that sensitive internal nomenclature never accidentally reaches customer-facing applications.

Integrating these curated glossaries into agentic engineering workflows offers substantial benefits for automated software development, compliance monitoring, and organizational governance. The glossaries guide code-generating models toward idiomatic company vocabulary, monitor interface labels for consistency, and explicitly flag deprecated terminology that should no longer be employed. Stored within version control systems and updated on a regular schedule through joint human-machine reviews, dynamic glossaries provide an indispensable foundation for enterprise domain modeling and accurate autonomous operations.
