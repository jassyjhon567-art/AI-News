Boeing Posts Broader Quarterly Loss as Air Force One Costs Mount

www.cnbc.com · Tue, 28 Jul 2026 13:56:52 GMT

Boeing reported a wider-than-expected quarterly loss after taking a $280 million charge on its delayed Air Force One program, driven by mounting fixed-price defense contract costs.

Aerospace giant Boeing reported a wider-than-expected quarterly financial loss, driven largely by ongoing cost overruns in its defense business. The company recorded a new $280 million charge related to the long-delayed Air Force One program, further dampening investor expectations and highlighting continuous operational challenges in fulfilling complex government defense commitments.

The Presidential Aircraft Replacement program, which involves converting two Boeing 747-8 airliners into specialized presidential transports, has faced prolonged delays, labor shortages, and rising manufacturing costs. Under a fixed-price contract structure negotiated with the U.S. government, Boeing is obligated to absorb any financial expenditures that exceed the agreed budget. These fixed-price defense contracts have repeatedly forced the company to take substantial write-downs, placing significant strain on the overall performance of its defense, space, and security unit.

Despite these financial setbacks, Boeing is actively working to stabilize its supply chain and streamline production processes across both its commercial and defense sectors. Company executives continue to emphasize rigorous quality controls and operational efficiency to mitigate future cost overruns. While the Air Force One program remains a visible drag on earnings, management expressed commitment to delivering the advanced aircraft while rebuilding financial health and restoring predictability across its operational footprint.
