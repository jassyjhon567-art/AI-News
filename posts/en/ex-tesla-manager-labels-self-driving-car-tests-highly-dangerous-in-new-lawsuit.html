Former Tesla Manager Files Lawsuit Alleging 'Highly Dangerous' Self-Driving Car Tests

www.slashgear.com · Wed, 29 Jul 2026 03:07:13 +0000

A former Tesla manager has filed a lawsuit alleging the company used 'highly dangerous' self-driving car testing procedures that endangered employees and the public.

A former Tesla manager has filed a comprehensive lawsuit against the electric vehicle manufacturer, alleging that the company engaged in "highly dangerous" testing procedures while developing its autonomous vehicle technology. The legal action sheds light on internal operational friction as Tesla aggressively pursues its corporate goal of achieving complete Full Self-Driving capabilities across its fleet. According to the court documents, corporate management routinely prioritized rapid software development cycles and aggressive rollout timelines over established industry safety protocols, creating what the plaintiff describes as an inherently hazardous testing environment.

The detailed complaint outlines serious allegations that safety warnings raised by internal engineering staff were systematically ignored or dismissed by senior leadership. Test personnel were allegedly instructed to execute unpredictable maneuvers on public roads without sufficient fallback safety mechanisms or adequate risk assessment procedures. The lawsuit asserts that these reckless practices not only imperiled the employees tasked with evaluating the software in real-world driving conditions, but also created unacceptable safety hazards for everyday pedestrians and other motorists sharing the public roadways.

This emerging legal battle adds significant pressure to Tesla at a time when the automaker already faces heightened scrutiny from federal safety regulators and public safety advocacy groups regarding its driver-assist software. Federal safety authorities have previously launched multiple high-profile investigations into traffic collisions linked to the company's advanced driver-assistance systems. The detailed claims made by this former manager could bolster existing governmental inquiries, potentially forcing the company to re-evaluate its software testing methodologies, corporate oversight, and public safety commitments moving forward.
