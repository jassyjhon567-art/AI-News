Global Hunger Decreases for Third Straight Year Despite Deepening Regional Disparities, UN Report Finds

www.who.int · Tue, 21 Jul 2026 08:26:17 Z

A new UN report reveals global hunger fell for a third straight year in 2025, though fragile progress and regional disparities threaten the goal of ending hunger by 2030.

Global hunger levels declined for the third consecutive year in 2025, offering a vital sign of hope that targeted international initiatives and policy interventions to combat food insecurity are yielding positive outcomes. According to The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2026 (SOFI 2026) report, jointly published on Tuesday by five specialized United Nations agencies, this sustained multi-year reduction clearly demonstrates that meaningful progress against global malnutrition is achievable when international assistance and local resource allocation are properly prioritized. The joint findings underscore the critical role of social protection programs and market stabilization policies in achieving these results.

However, UN experts caution that these global improvements remain dangerously fragile and disproportionately distributed across different geographical regions. While significant progress was recorded in several developing nations owing to economic recovery, improved supply chains, and strong agricultural yields, severe food insecurity continues to deepen in other vulnerable areas. Ongoing regional conflicts, devastating climate shocks, and persistent economic inflation continue to erode food access for millions of people, worsening the disparities between stabilizing regions and those trapped in acute humanitarian crises.

Ultimately, the SOFI 2026 report emphasizes that the trajectory of current progress remains vastly insufficient to meet the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals, particularly the ambitious objective of completely eradicating global hunger and malnutrition by the year 2030. To protect recent gains and address persistent structural inequities, international leaders, humanitarian organizations, and policymakers must urgently scale up targeted financing, reform trade policies, and strengthen climate-resilient local food production capabilities in the world's most severely vulnerable communities.
