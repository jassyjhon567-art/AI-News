HR Giant SHRM Ordered to Pay $400,000 After Revoking Job Offer Over Service Dog

www.inc.com · Tue, 13 Oct 2026 15:56:06 -0400

A jury ordered HR giant SHRM to pay $400,000 after finding it violated disability laws by rescinding a job offer when an applicant requested a medical service dog.

The Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM), one of the world's largest professional organizations dedicated to setting workplace human resource standards, has been ordered by a jury to pay $400,000 in damages. The verdict follows a legal ruling determining that the influential industry group violated disability protection laws by unlawfully withdrawing an employment offer from a qualified applicant who required a medical accommodation.

According to the lawsuit, the conflict began shortly after the prospective employee received the job offer and subsequently requested permission to bring a trained medical service dog to the office as an accommodation for her disability. Instead of entering into an interactive dialogue to explore how the accommodation could be safely implemented, the organization abruptly rescinded the offer. Jurors concluded that this sudden reversal breached disability nondiscrimination statutes, which require employers to evaluate accommodation requests in good faith rather than dismissing applicants outright.

The verdict carries significant symbolic weight throughout the corporate sector, as SHRM frequently counsels businesses nationwide on labor compliance, inclusive workplace policies, and proper hiring conduct. Industry analysts emphasize that the substantial penalty serves as a powerful reminder that every employer, regardless of its reputation or expertise in workforce management, must strictly uphold disability rights and maintain fair recruitment practices.
