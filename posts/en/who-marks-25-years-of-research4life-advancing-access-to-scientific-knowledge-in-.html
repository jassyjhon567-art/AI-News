WHO Celebrates 25 Years of Research4Life, Expanding Scientific Access Across 120 Countries

www.who.int · Mon, 20 Jul 2026 15:34:49 Z

The World Health Organization is celebrating 25 years of Research4Life, a public-private partnership providing researchers in over 120 countries with affordable access to scientific knowledge.

The World Health Organization (WHO) is officially marking the 25th anniversary of Research4Life, a pioneering public-private partnership designed to bridge the scientific knowledge gap in low- and middle-income countries. Launched to address severe inequalities in access to peer-reviewed scientific research, the initiative provides researchers, healthcare professionals, educators, and policy-makers with free or highly affordable access to academic literature. Over the past quarter-century, the program has expanded dramatically, now serving institutions across more than 120 eligible countries worldwide.

By delivering critical access to major scientific databases and thousands of peer-reviewed journals, Research4Life has fundamentally transformed the capacity of developing nations to address local and global challenges. Access to robust scientific evidence enables medical practitioners to enhance patient care, assists academics in conducting high-impact research, and guides government leaders in formulating effective, evidence-based public health policies. The partnership encompasses specialized programs covering diverse disciplines, including health, agriculture, the environment, law, and social sciences.

As the initiative celebrates this significant milestone, the WHO and its partner organizations emphasize that equitable access to scientific knowledge remains vital for sustainable global development. By removing financial barriers to scholarly publishing, Research4Life fosters local research ecosystems and empowers researchers in lower-income nations to contribute actively to global scientific discourse. Looking to the future, the partnership aims to continue expanding its reach, ensuring that vital health and scientific insights remain accessible to all who require them.
