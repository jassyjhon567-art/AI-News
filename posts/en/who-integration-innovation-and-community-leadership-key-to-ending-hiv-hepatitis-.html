WHO Emphasizes Integration, Innovation, and Community Leadership to Eliminate HIV, Hepatitis, and STIs

www.who.int · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 15:45:12 Z

A new WHO report highlights that integrating health services, advancing innovation, and empowering communities are essential to overcoming global challenges and eliminating HIV, viral hepatitis, and STIs by 2030.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has officially unveiled a comprehensive progress report on the Global Health Sector Strategies (GHSS) for HIV, viral hepatitis, and sexually transmitted infections (STIs) covering the 2022–2030 period. Timed to coincide with the 26th International AIDS Conference and ahead of World Hepatitis Day on July 28, the document rigorously evaluates global efforts to curb these persistent public health threats. It underscores that while substantial strides have been made in expanding life-saving treatment and diagnostic access, systemic barriers continue to impede universal progress.

According to the new assessment, achieving the ambitious 2030 elimination targets will rely heavily on three core pillars: primary care service integration, technological innovation, and empowered community-led initiatives. Integrating screening, prevention, and treatment for HIV, viral hepatitis, and STIs into routine primary healthcare systems has proven vital for reaching marginalized and vulnerable populations. Simultaneously, breakthroughs in diagnostic tools, long-acting therapeutics, and novel prevention strategies are creating unprecedented opportunities to interrupt transmission chains more efficiently across diverse international healthcare settings.

Despite these significant technological and structural advancements, the WHO warns that persistent funding gaps, social stigma, and structural inequities threaten to undermine global momentum. The report calls on international partners, policymakers, and national governments to prioritize sustainable health financing and fully support local communities, who remain at the forefront of advocacy and direct service delivery. Without urgent, coordinated action to scale up equitable health solutions worldwide, millions of individuals will remain at risk of preventable infections and severe health complications.
