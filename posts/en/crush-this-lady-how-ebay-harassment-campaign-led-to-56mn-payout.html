How eBay's Bizarre Cyberstalking Campaign Against a Boston Couple Ended in a $56 Million Settlement

www.ft.com · Sun, 02 Aug 2026 12:00:05 GMT

eBay reached a landmark $56 million civil settlement with a Boston couple targeted by company executives in a terrifying cyberstalking and physical harassment campaign involving macabre deliveries.

A terrifying corporate harassment campaign conducted by former eBay employees against a Massachusetts couple has culminated in a historic $56 million settlement. David and Ina Steiner, who ran a specialized online newsletter focusing on ecommerce news and seller issues, became targets of high-level company executives after publishing articles that criticized eBay's business practices. What began as internal corporate frustration quickly escalated into an organized campaign involving heavy online surveillance, public doxxing, and abusive threats aimed at intimidating the couple into silence.

The relentless intimidation extended far beyond digital space, rapidly spilling into deeply disturbing physical tactics designed to instill fear. The Boston-area couple began receiving a series of macabre and unsettling packages delivered directly to their residential home. These packages included a bloody pig mask, a box of live cockroaches, a funeral wreath, and explicit books on surviving the loss of a spouse. Furthermore, former security personnel from the company traveled to Massachusetts to directly surveil the couple's home, attempting to place a tracking device on their personal vehicle.

Following an extensive federal investigation into the matter, multiple former eBay employees and high-ranking security executives faced serious criminal charges, with several ultimately receiving federal prison sentences for their actions. While the company had previously paid a separate multi-million-dollar criminal penalty to government authorities, this landmark civil settlement directly resolves the Steiners' civil lawsuit against the e-commerce giant and the individuals involved, officially closing a chapter on one of the most shocking corporate intimidation scandals in corporate history.
