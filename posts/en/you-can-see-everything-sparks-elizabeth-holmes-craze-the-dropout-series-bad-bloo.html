Anticipation for New Elizabeth Holmes Documentary Drives Renewed Interest in Past Media

www.forbes.com · Sat, 17 Oct 2026 10:00:00 -0400

Upcoming documentary "You Can See Everything" has sparked renewed engagement with previous Elizabeth Holmes media, including Hulu's series "The Dropout" and John Carreyrou's book "Bad Blood."

Anticipation surrounding Nathan Fielder’s upcoming documentary about Elizabeth Holmes, titled "You Can See Everything," has reignited widespread public fascination with the disgraced Theranos founder. As buzz builds ahead of the project’s release, audiences are revisiting the dramatic saga of one of Silicon Valley’s most infamous corporate collapses. The announcement of the new film has prompted viewers and readers to re-examine the intricate web of deception that defined Holmes’s failed biotech enterprise.

This renewed curiosity has triggered a significant resurgence in engagement for older Holmes-related media. Hulu’s award-winning drama series "The Dropout," which dramatized the entrepreneur's early ambitions and subsequent legal reckoning, has seen a sharp increase in viewership across streaming platforms. Concurrently, investigative journalist John Carreyrou’s definitive book, "Bad Blood: Secrets and Lies in a Silicon Valley Startup," is experiencing a fresh wave of reader interest. Carreyrou’s original reporting for The Wall Street Journal played a critical role in unraveling Theranos's fraudulent claims, and his detailed account remains a key touchstone for those tracking the saga.

The resurgence of these materials highlights the enduring cultural grip of the Theranos scandal, which exposed deep vulnerabilities within tech startup culture and investor optimism. As audiences prepare for Fielder’s distinct perspective in "You Can See Everything," the renewed engagement across streaming and publishing sectors proves that major new releases can breathe fresh life into existing catalogs. Rather than competing, past and present accounts are complementing one another as the public continues to analyze one of the most high-profile corporate downfalls in modern history.
