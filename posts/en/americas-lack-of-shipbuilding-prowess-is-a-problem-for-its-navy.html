Declining Shipbuilding Capacity Poses Strategic Challenges for the U.S. Navy

www.economist.com · Thu, 06 Aug 2026 13:00:25 +0000

The decline of domestic shipbuilding severely undermines U.S. Navy readiness, while China’s dominant commercial shipyards afford Beijing a major strategic advantage in maritime power.

The United States Navy is facing increasingly severe strategic challenges driven by the long-term decline of the domestic shipbuilding industry. Over the past several decades, American commercial shipbuilding has contracted significantly, leaving the nation dependent on a small, consolidated network of specialized defense shipyards. This loss of broader industrial capacity has led to prolonged maintenance backlogs, project delays, and rising costs, severely constraining the Navy’s ability to construct new vessels or efficiently maintain its current fleet during a period of heightened geopolitical tensions.

Meanwhile, China has successfully built a massive maritime manufacturing ecosystem by leveraging a dominant commercial shipbuilding sector. Chinese shipyards lead the global market in commercial vessel construction, benefiting from vast industrial scale, state-of-the-art technological infrastructure, and a huge pool of skilled labor. This booming commercial foundation directly supports naval expansion, allowing Beijing to produce complex warships faster and more cost-effectively, while simultaneously maintaining a robust capacity to repair and surge maritime forces during sustained conflicts.

To counter this growing disparity, defense analysts and U.S. policymakers emphasize the urgent need to revitalize the national maritime industrial base. Rebuilding domestic capability will require substantial long-term financial investments, modernizing aging shipyard infrastructure, and overcoming critical labor shortages in specialized engineering and trade disciplines. Without significant structural reforms and a renewed national focus on maritime industrial strength, the U.S. Navy risks compromising its global strategic dominance and operational readiness against rapidly advancing rival powers.
