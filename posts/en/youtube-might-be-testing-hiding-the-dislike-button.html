YouTube May Be Testing Hiding the Dislike Button in Video Interface

www.theverge.com · 2026-10-20T20:00:56-04:00

YouTube appears to be testing an interface redesign that moves or hides the dislike button inside an overflow menu for select users.

YouTube appears to be testing significant changes to its user interface that involve hiding the dislike button on standard video pages. According to a recent report by 9to5Google, several viewers have noticed that the familiar thumbs-down icon is no longer directly visible in its traditional spot beside the like button beneath the video player. This subtle design experiment suggests that the Google-owned video-sharing platform is evaluating new ways to organize primary viewer interactions and engagement tools.

Evidence of the ongoing trial surfaced across various online forums and social media platforms. Multiple users on X shared screenshots revealing that the dislike button had been moved entirely out of direct sight and into the three-dots overflow menu alongside other secondary options. Furthermore, a user on Reddit reported an interface where the button appeared to be completely missing. Because these modifications have been spotted by only a limited group of people, the platform appears to be conducting a controlled A/B test to gauge reactions.

This development follows a major controversial policy shift in late 2021, when YouTube officially hid public dislike counts to protect content creators from targeted harassment campaigns. While creators retain access to dislike metrics in their private analytics, regular viewers can no longer easily evaluate community sentiment prior to watching. Tucking the button away inside a nested menu could further minimize casual negative engagement, though YouTube has not yet confirmed whether this redesign will permanently roll out globally to all users in the future.
