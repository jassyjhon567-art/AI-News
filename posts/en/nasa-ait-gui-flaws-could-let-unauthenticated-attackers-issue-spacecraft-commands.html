Critical Vulnerability in NASA's AIT-GUI Could Allow Attackers to Send Unauthorized Spacecraft Commands

thehackernews.com · Thu, 20 Aug 2026 16:35:11 +0530

Security researchers have uncovered a critical vulnerability in NASA's open-source AIT-GUI, enabling unauthenticated attackers to execute unauthorized commands on spacecraft and instrument systems.

Security researchers at Cycode have uncovered a critical vulnerability chain in AIT-GUI, the browser-based operator console for NASA and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory's (JPL) open-source AMMOS Instrument Toolkit. The discovered flaw could permit an unauthenticated remote attacker to issue arbitrary commands directly into the core spacecraft and scientific instrument command bus, potentially compromising critical mission interfaces.

Tracked under the advisory identifier GHSA-p9r8-2q67-fp86, the security issue has been assigned a severe CVSS v3.1 score of 9.4 out of 10. The toolkit is specifically engineered to handle down-link and up-link telemetry for instrument suites and ground data systems. By chaining vulnerabilities within the graphical interface, threat actors could bypass standard authentication mechanisms and disrupt telemetry processing or tamper with ground-to-spacecraft operations.

Because the AMMOS Instrument Toolkit is utilized across mission-critical developments and aerospace research environments, securing the software is vital. Engineering teams and organizations operating deployments of AIT-GUI are advised to evaluate their installations, deploy the appropriate security patches, and enforce strict network segmentation to safeguard ground control assets from unauthorized network access.
