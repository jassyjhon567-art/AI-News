Google Nest Hub Sleep Sensing to Transition from Google Fit to New Health App

9to5google.com · Fri, 31 Jul 2026 19:38:15 +0000

Google is migrating the Soli-powered Sleep Sensing feature on the second-generation Nest Hub from Google Fit to its new Health app to consolidate user wellness data.

Google is officially preparing to transition the Sleep Sensing features on its second-generation Nest Hub smart display from the legacy Google Fit platform to its new Health application. Since the device was first introduced, the innovative contact-free sleep tracking technology—which utilizes Soli radar sensors to monitor nighttime rest—has exclusively synchronized its gathered data with Google Fit. However, as Google gradually sunsets the older fitness application in favor of a more comprehensive and modern health ecosystem, a shift in data management for smart display owners has become essential.

The low-power Soli radar chip embedded inside the second-generation Nest Hub enables the device to accurately track a user's sleep stages, breathing habits, and various ambient disruptions such as coughing or snoring without requiring any wearable accessories or intrusive cameras. Positioned on a nightstand, the display analyzes subtle movements and audio cues to deliver detailed nightly sleep reports. Until now, these personalized metrics were stored and reviewed inside Google Fit, providing users with a combined view of their sleep quality alongside daily activity levels.

By moving Nest Hub Sleep Sensing into the dedicated Health app, Google aims to deliver a far more cohesive wellness experience across its entire ecosystem. This transition is expected to allow users to combine ambient sleep data with information collected from other connected fitness devices and smartwatches in one centralized dashboard. Current Nest Hub owners will likely experience a seamless data migration process, ensuring that their historical sleep logs and personal insights remain preserved as Google continues to refine its digital health strategies.
