WHO Expresses Deep Regret Over Official Notification of US Withdrawal

www.who.int · Sat, 24 Jan 2026 20:09:49 Z

The World Health Organization has expressed regret over the United States' formal withdrawal notification, warning that the decision compromises global safety ahead of upcoming high-level governance reviews.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has officially expressed its profound regret following the formal notification regarding the intended withdrawal of the United States from the international health body. In its public statement, the organization highlighted that this monumental decision poses severe risks to public health preparedness, ultimately rendering both the United States and the global community less safe from ongoing and future health crises. Over the decades, the partnership between the United States and the global agency has played a fundamental role in eradicating diseases, responding to pandemics, and establishing robust global health infrastructure.

The formal notification of departure introduces a multitude of procedural, financial, and operational challenges that require thorough deliberation among global health governance bodies. According to the health agency, the critical issues stemming from this decision will be formally considered during upcoming institutional assemblies. The WHO Executive Board is scheduled to examine the strategic and operational implications of the withdrawal at its regular meeting beginning on February 2, where representatives from member states will assess the immediate impact on ongoing global health initiatives.

Looking further ahead, the long-term ramifications of the decision will be addressed at the highest level during the World Health Assembly's annual session in May 2026. Member nations will gather to evaluate how to maintain essential disease surveillance, emergency response capabilities, and universal health programs in the wake of this transition. Despite these governance challenges, the World Health Organization reaffirmed its dedication to serving all people globally and fostering international solidarity to safeguard public health across borders.
