Figma Slows Hiring Due to AI Tools, but Heavy Spending Spooks Investors as Shares Tumble

www.fastcompany.com · 2026-08-06T13:02:00

Figma shares plummeted over 14 percent as soaring R&D costs and AI spending alarmed investors, despite revenue growth, raised 2026 guidance, and reduced hiring via AI efficiencies.

Design software provider Figma saw its shares plummet by more than 14 percent in premarket trading following its second-quarter earnings report, reflecting growing investor skepticism over heavy artificial intelligence investments. Although the company posted a strong 48 percent year-over-year revenue increase to $370.1 million, the surge in top-line growth was overshadowed by a GAAP operating loss of $117.3 million. Total operating expenses nearly doubled compared to the previous year, reaching $426.9 million, driven primarily by research and development costs that surged to $167.3 million from $83.1 million a year earlier.

In an attempt to address cost concerns, Figma executives highlighted how artificial intelligence is helping curb internal overhead expenses. During the post-earnings conference call, Chief Financial Officer Praveer Melwani stated that the company is currently hiring fewer people than originally anticipated. He explained that AI tools have enabled Figma to augment its existing workforce and modernize internal processes across the board. The quarter was also notable as the first to fully incorporate revenue from AI credit monetization, underscoring the company’s efforts to turn its technology investments into practical products and operational efficiencies.

Demonstrating confidence in its long-term trajectory, Figma raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to a range of $1.463 billion to $1.467 billion, representing an average annual growth rate of 39 percent. However, the upgraded outlook did little to appease cautious Wall Street investors who are increasingly nervous about heavy AI expenditure across the tech sector. Figma’s stock is down 25 percent this year and nearly 69 percent over the past 12 months, continuing a volatile trend since its highly anticipated initial public offering roughly a year ago.
