Fossilized Spines of Saber-Toothed Cats Reveal Signs of Inbreeding Before Extinction

www.sciencenews.org · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 04:00:00 +0000

Fossilized spines of saber-toothed cats from the La Brea Tar Pits reveal skeletal deformities and tumors, suggesting severe inbreeding contributed to the species' eventual extinction.

Fossilized remains of saber-toothed cats excavated from the renowned La Brea Tar Pits in Los Angeles are offering paleontologists unprecedented insights into the final days of these prehistoric apex predators. Recent detailed anatomical analyses of their preserved spinal columns revealed a surprisingly high frequency of debilitating skeletal abnormalities, including fused vertebrae, deformed spinal structures, and rare bone tumors. These physical defects strongly suggest that the species was suffering from severe genetic distress as its population rapidly declined toward the end of the Pleistocene epoch.

Researchers examining the extensive fossil collection noted that the observed spinal deformities closely mirror medical conditions seen in modern isolated animal populations suffering from inbreeding. As climate shifts and prey shortages fragmented their vast natural habitats, these massive cats likely became restricted to smaller, isolated geographic pockets. The resulting reduction in genetic diversity exacerbated hereditary health problems, making the animals increasingly vulnerable to physical limitations and chronic pain that severely compromised their hunting efficiency and overall survival.

While severe inbreeding alone may not have been the single catalyst for their ultimate extinction, these groundbreaking findings indicate that genetic deterioration significantly compromised the species' resilience. Trapped in a perilous cycle of shrinking populations, physical impairment, and declining genetic health, the formidable apex predators were ultimately unable to adapt to a rapidly changing environmental landscape. Paleontologists emphasize that this discovery underscores the critical importance of genetic diversity in species survival, offering valuable insights for contemporary wildlife conservation.
