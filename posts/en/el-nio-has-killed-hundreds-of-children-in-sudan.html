Severe El Niño Drought Triggers Deadly Hunger and Disease Crisis for Sudanese Children

www.newscientist.com · Fri, 16 Oct 2026 16:37:49 +0000

An intense El Niño-driven drought in Sudan has killed hundreds of children as extreme food and water shortages fuel severe malnutrition and preventable diseases.

A severe and prolonged drought driven by the El Niño weather phenomenon has unleashed an escalating humanitarian crisis across Sudan, resulting in the tragic deaths of hundreds of children. The extreme weather pattern has disrupted regular rainfall cycles, devastating agricultural output and drying up vital pastures throughout the country. As staple crop harvests fail and local food reserves become exhausted, millions of vulnerable families are left facing acute hunger, with young children suffering the most immediate and severe consequences of malnutrition.

Beyond the widespread hunger, the environmental shock has triggered a catastrophic water crisis that is accelerating the spread of life-threatening illnesses. As natural water points dry up, rural and displaced populations are increasingly forced to depend on unsafe, contaminated sources. This lack of potable water and proper sanitation has led to widespread outbreaks of preventable diseases, such as cholera and diarrhea. Local health clinics, already operating with minimal supplies, are being overwhelmed by an influx of critically ill infants and children who require urgent medical stabilization.

Humanitarian organizations are sounding the alarm over the deepening tragedy, emphasizing that the window to avert even wider loss of life is rapidly closing. The compounded impacts of climate shocks, food deficits, and an overstretched healthcare system have pushed vulnerable communities to the brink of survival. Relief agencies are urging the international community to swiftly expand funding and logistical support for emergency food aid, clean water delivery, and therapeutic feeding programs before the catastrophe claims countless more young lives.
