World's Smallest Crocodiles Seen Snatching Bats in Underground Caves in Never-Before-Seen Behavior

www.livescience.com · Thu, 01 Oct 2026 16:10:41 +0000

Remarkable new footage reveals Gabon's cave-dwelling dwarf crocodiles hunting bats in their dark underground habitat, documenting a predatory behavior never recorded before.

Gabon's mysterious cave-dwelling dwarf crocodiles have been captured on camera exhibiting an extraordinary and previously undocumented hunting behavior. In an environment shrouded in complete darkness, these rare reptiles were filmed snatching roosting and flying bats straight out of the cave air and off cavern walls. The remarkable footage, released ahead of the premiere of the natural history series "Africa: Earth's Wild Home," provides a rare glimpse into the secret lives of these isolated subterranean predators.

Dwarf crocodiles are recognized as the smallest living crocodile species in the world, typically inhabiting tropical swamps and slow-moving rivers across Central and West Africa. However, the unique population found in Gabon's remote cave networks has adapted to a life largely cut off from the outside world. Living in total subterranean isolation and navigating pools often laden with bat guano, these specialized reptiles have developed distinct physical traits and behaviors to endure one of the most challenging environments on the planet.

The newly observed predation tactic demonstrates how resourceful apex predators can become when adapting to unconventional ecosystems. Because typical aquatic prey such as fish and freshwater crabs are exceedingly scarce inside the deep cave system, the crocodiles have turned toward the abundant bat colonies sharing their underground shelter. Biologists and wildlife filmmakers emphasize that documenting this bat-snatching behavior underscores the incredible resilience of wildlife and highlights how many natural wonders still remain hidden from modern science.
