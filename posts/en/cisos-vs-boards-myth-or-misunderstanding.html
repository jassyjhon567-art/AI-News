CISOs and Corporate Boards: Bridging the Cybersecurity Communication Gap

www.darkreading.com · Fri, 24 Jul 2026 21:31:53 GMT

While rising cyber threats force corporate boards to prioritize security, ongoing communication barriers with CISOs highlight the urgent need for a shared language and aligned strategic goals.

As cyber threats rapidly escalate in scale, complexity, and frequency, corporate boards are increasingly forced to elevate cybersecurity from a backroom IT issue to a top-tier strategic agenda item. Despite this heightened urgency, a fundamental communication gap continues to persist between Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs) and corporate directors. While both groups share the common goal of protecting organizational assets, their differing operational backgrounds and strategic perspectives often result in misaligned priorities and ongoing frustration.

At the core of this divide is a significant disconnect in how risk is communicated and measured. Security executives typically present risks through detailed technical metrics, tactical threat intelligence, and complex terminology, which can obscure the broader strategic implications. In contrast, corporate board members evaluate risk through the lens of financial exposure, regulatory compliance, operational business continuity, and overall brand reputation. Without a shared language to bridge this gap, boards frequently struggle to assess the value of security investments, while CISOs feel that critical warnings are overlooked.

Bridging this divide requires dedicated effort and mutual support from both corporate oversight bodies and cybersecurity leadership teams. Recent industry observations suggest that both sides recognize their limitations and are actively seeking improved frameworks to enhance collaboration. CISOs must refine their ability to articulate technical vulnerabilities in terms of business impact, while board members must proactively build their digital literacy to ask the right strategic questions. By establishing clear channels of structured, continuous dialogue, modern organizations can successfully turn a longstanding misunderstanding into a robust and cohesive enterprise-wide defense strategy.
