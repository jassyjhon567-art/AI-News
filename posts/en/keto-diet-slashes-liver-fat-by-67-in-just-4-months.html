Keto Diet Reduces Liver Fat by 67% in Four Months, Outperforming Other Diets, Study Finds

www.sciencedaily.com · Fri, 20 Nov 2026 07:44:39 EST

A new clinical trial shows that the ketogenic diet reduces liver fat by 67 percent and reverses prediabetes in roughly half of participants within five months.

A recent clinical trial has revealed that adopting a ketogenic diet can dramatically decrease liver fat while substantially improving markers of metabolic health. When evaluated alongside traditional Mediterranean and standard low-fat dietary patterns, the low-carbohydrate, high-fat protocol demonstrated clearly superior outcomes in purging hepatic fat deposits. Intriguingly, these metabolic benefits were recorded even among participants who shed comparable amounts of total body weight, suggesting that macronutrient distribution plays a critical role independent of weight loss alone.

Over an intervention period lasting roughly four to five months, participants strictly following the ketogenic regimen experienced an impressive average reduction in liver fat of 67 percent. Beyond targeting hepatic steatosis, the diet also catalyzed profound systemic benefits for blood sugar regulation. Researchers discovered that approximately half of the keto participants who initially showed signs of prediabetes successfully reversed their status, no longer meeting the clinical diagnostic criteria for the metabolic condition by the trial's conclusion.

The study's outcomes offer valuable insights into the nutritional management of metabolic dysfunction and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, a growing global health concern. While healthcare professionals have long debated the long-term sustainability of various popular diets, these results emphasize that deliberate carbohydrate restriction can trigger rapid therapeutic changes within the liver. By directly targeting intrahepatic fat and stabilizing insulin dynamics, the keto approach emerges as a potent non-pharmacological strategy for metabolic rehabilitation.
