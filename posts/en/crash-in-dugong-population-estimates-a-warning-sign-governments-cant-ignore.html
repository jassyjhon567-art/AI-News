Sharp Decline in Dugong Population Sparks Urgent Calls for Government Action

phys.org · Wed, 02 Sep 2026 22:20:04 EDT

A 50 percent decline in Gulf of Carpentaria dugong estimates underscores an urgent need for government action and collaborative conservation efforts to protect the vulnerable marine species.

A dramatic 50 percent decline in estimated dugong populations across the Gulf of Carpentaria has sounded serious alarm bells among conservationists, scientists, and environmental advocates. This troubling trend was identified through comprehensive aerial and marine surveys carried out collaboratively by researchers from James Cook University, Charles Darwin University, and dedicated Indigenous Ranger groups. The alarming data reveals that one of the region's most iconic marine species is facing an unprecedented downturn, placing immense and immediate pressure on northern Australia's coastal biodiversity.

Dugongs, often known as sea cows, play an indispensable ecological role in sustaining the health of coastal ecosystems, particularly the extensive seagrass meadows upon which they graze. The research partnership brought together advanced scientific methodologies and the profound traditional ecological knowledge held by Indigenous Rangers, providing an accurate and sobering assessment of remote marine habitats. Marine scientists emphasize that a population drop of this magnitude is a stark wake-up call, suggesting that cumulative environmental disturbances, water quality issues, and changing habitat conditions are severely impacting these gentle marine mammals.

Conservation leaders and researchers stress that governments can no longer afford to overlook these critical warning signs. Immediate, coordinated policy action is urgently required to enforce stronger marine habitat protections, regulate coastal activities, and bolster long-term preservation efforts across the Gulf. Supporting sustained partnerships with Indigenous Rangers and scientific institutions will be essential to mitigating threats and tracking future recovery. Without decisive and prompt interventions, the survival of the Gulf of Carpentaria's dugong population remains perilously uncertain, threatening the broader balance of Australia's northern marine heritage.
