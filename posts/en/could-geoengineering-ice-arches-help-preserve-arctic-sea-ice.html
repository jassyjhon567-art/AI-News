Could Geoengineering 'Ice Arches' Help Preserve Arctic Sea Ice?

www.newscientist.com · Fri, 07 Aug 2026 11:00:00 +0000

Researchers are exploring whether artificially thickening Arctic ice arches between Canada and Greenland can prevent older sea ice from drifting south and melting.

Scientists and climate researchers are exploring innovative geoengineering concepts to combat the accelerating loss of Arctic sea ice driven by global warming. Among these ambitious proposals is the idea of artificially strengthening and thickening naturally occurring "ice arches"—massive frozen blockages that form within narrow polar straits, particularly between northern Canada and Greenland. These natural barriers play a critical role in stabilizing the polar cap by preventing older, thicker sea ice from drifting southward into warmer ocean waters where it rapidly melts.

The Nares Strait serves as one of the primary pathways through which multi-year Arctic ice escapes into the North Atlantic Ocean. In recent years, rising atmospheric and ocean temperatures have caused these natural ice arches to collapse much earlier in the season than historical norms. Proponents of this geoengineering strategy suggest that targeted human interventions, such as pumping seawater onto existing ice sheets during the freezing winter months to increase their thickness and structural resilience, could help reinforce these crucial arches and significantly prolong their lifespan.

Despite the potential advantages of preserving vital polar ice, the concept faces immense technical, ecological, and geopolitical hurdles. Critics caution that large-scale technological interventions in fragile Arctic environments could lead to unintended ecological disruptions, affecting marine ecosystems and indigenous communities reliant on predictable ice dynamics. Ultimately, scientists emphasize that while localized engineering solutions might buy precious time for vulnerable ecosystems, they cannot replace fundamental global action aimed at drastically reducing greenhouse gas emissions and addressing the root causes of climate change.
