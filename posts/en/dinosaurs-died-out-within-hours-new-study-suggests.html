Dinosaurs May Have Perished Within Hours Following Massive Asteroid Impact, Study Suggests

www.popsci.com · Tue, 28 Jul 2026 09:00:00 -0400

A new study suggests the Chicxulub asteroid impact caused a massive thermal radiation pulse that ignited global wildfires, killing most dinosaurs within hours of collision.

A groundbreaking scientific study suggests that non-avian dinosaurs may have perished within just a few hours following the catastrophic Chicxulub asteroid impact, challenging long-held theories that their extinction was a gradual process extending over thousands of years. The massive celestial body, which struck Earth approximately 66 million years ago, unleashed an unimaginable amount of kinetic energy that rapidly transformed the entire planet into an inferno.

According to the researchers, the initial collision ejected vast quantities of vaporized rock and molten debris high above Earth's atmosphere. As these microscopic particles cooled and fell back toward the planet at hypervelocity, their high-speed re-entry generated an overwhelming thermal radiation pulse. This intense pulse effectively turned the atmosphere into a planetary broiler, raising global surface temperatures to scorching levels and igniting widespread wildfires that engulfed forests and surface life across the globe in a matter of hours.

While secondary environmental consequences—such as lingering dust clouds, prolonged freezing temperatures, and acid rain—undoubtedly contributed to the long-term demise of remaining organisms, the initial heatwave proved immediately fatal for most large land animals. The research offers a dramatic new perspective on prehistoric mass extinction, demonstrating how a single cosmic collision could reshape Earth's biological history almost overnight and eliminate millions of years of evolutionary progress in a single afternoon.
