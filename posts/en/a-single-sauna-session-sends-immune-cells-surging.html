Single Sauna Session Boosts Immune Cells, Study Finds

www.sciencedaily.com · Mon, 27 Jul 2026 21:20:30 EDT

A study by Finnish researchers reveals that a 30-minute sauna session followed by a cold shower temporarily increases white blood cells, putting the immune system on high alert.

A single 30-minute sauna session, particularly when paired with a brief cold shower, can temporarily place the human immune system on a higher state of alert, according to recent research from Finland. The study revealed that exposure to intense heat followed by quick cooling triggers an immediate physiological reaction, causing protective immune cells to surge through the bloodstream in response to the rapid temperature shift.

During the investigation, scientists observed significant increases across virtually every category of circulating white blood cells right after the sauna routine. Key infection fighters, including neutrophils and lymphocytes, showed particularly elevated numbers. Neutrophils serve as the primary rapid-response units against invading bacterial pathogens, whereas lymphocytes play a fundamental role in coordinating targeted immune defenses. This sudden influx indicates that the temporary physical stress induced by extreme thermal variation actively stimulates the rapid mobilization of these critical defense cells.

While the immediate surge in immune cells is striking, medical experts note that this spike represents a temporary acute stress response rather than a permanent improvement in long-term immunity. The cell counts gradually return to normal baseline levels as the body cools down and stabilizes. Nevertheless, because sauna bathing has long been celebrated in traditional Finnish culture for its broad health benefits, these findings offer intriguing biological evidence of how controlled thermal therapy might help prime the immune system for dynamic responses.
