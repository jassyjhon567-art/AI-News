Seven Peculiar Pets in History: From a Beer-Drinking Elk to the Pope’s Elephant

www.popsci.com · Sun, 02 Aug 2026 08:00:00 -0400

Throughout history, notable figures from astronomers to popes have kept eccentric pets, including beer-drinking elks, white elephants, and alligators, captivating the public across centuries.

While dogs and cats have long held the title of humanity's favorite companions, history is filled with eccentric figures who chose far more unconventional animals to share their lives. From royalty and world leaders to famous scholars, prominent individuals throughout the ages have welcomed exotic and unusual creatures into their homes and royal courts. These extraordinary animal companions left behind fascinating tales that highlight the weird and wonderful side of human history.

One legendary story involves Tycho Brahe, the famous 16th-century Danish astronomer, who famously kept a pet elk. The animal reportedly wandered freely around his castle and met a tragic end after consuming large quantities of beer at a banquet and tumbling down a flight of stairs. Around the same era, Pope Leo X was gifted a rare white elephant named Hanno from Portugal. The magnificent creature quickly became the star of Rome, participating in public processions and captivating the Italian populace before its untimely death.

Other notable historical animal enthusiasts include United States President John Quincy Adams, who reportedly kept an alligator in the East Room of the White House, and the Romantic poet Lord Byron, who brought a domesticated bear to reside with him at Cambridge University. These unusual choices demonstrate how status, wealth, and eccentricity often granted historic figures the liberty to keep extraordinary pets, creating stories that continue to fascinate historians and animal lovers today.
