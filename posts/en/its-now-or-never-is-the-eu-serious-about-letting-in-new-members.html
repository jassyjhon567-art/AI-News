It’s Now or Never: Is the European Union Truly Ready for Enlargement?

www.ft.com · Fri, 07 Aug 2026 04:00:17 GMT

As geopolitical shifts reignite the debate over European Union expansion, candidate nations demand progress while Brussels grapples with internal reform and membership uncertainty.

For years, candidate nations across the Western Balkans and Eastern Europe have remained in a diplomatic holding pattern, endlessly awaiting significant movement toward European Union membership. Recently, however, dramatic geopolitical shifts have transformed the enlargement debate into an urgent priority. Leaders from aspirant countries argue that the present moment offers a critical window of opportunity, warning that prolonged indecision could permanently damage trust in the accession process and leave regional security increasingly vulnerable to external pressures.

The ongoing conflict in Ukraine has fundamentally re-energized discussions surrounding EU expansion, reframing enlargement as a vital strategic necessity rather than merely a routine technical procedure. Proponents contend that incorporating neighboring democracies is essential for reinforcing regional stability, driving institutional reform, and safeguarding core European values. By securing these nations within the European fold, the bloc can effectively counter competing global influences and foster long-term economic resilience along its eastern and southern borders.

Despite this renewed momentum, deep-seated skepticism persists among several established member states and key officials in Brussels. Critics emphasize that comprehensive internal governance reforms must precede any further expansion, as the union already faces internal challenges regarding unanimous decision-making and the rule of law. Until candidate countries achieve full alignment with EU standards and the bloc resolves its own institutional capacity issues, the path toward welcoming new members will remain highly complex and uncertain.
