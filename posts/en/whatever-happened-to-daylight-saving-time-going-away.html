Why Is Daylight Saving Time Still Here Despite Moves to Abolish It?

lifehacker.com · Fri, 16 Oct 2026 17:30:00 +0000

Despite congressional efforts and public support to eliminate the biannual time change, legislative gridlock and disagreements over permanent time standards have kept Daylight Saving Time in place.

Every year, millions of people brace themselves for the disruptive biannual ritual of shifting their clocks forward and backward. Not long ago, it appeared that this century-old practice was finally on its way out. Lawmakers in Congress took historic steps toward eliminating the routine clock change, advancing legislation aimed at bringing an end to the tradition and establishing a single, consistent time standard year-round.

However, despite initial momentum and broad public weariness with changing clocks, the initiative stalled before becoming law. While the proposal found support in the Senate, it failed to advance through the House of Representatives. Lawmakers and experts became divided over which standard to adopt permanently. Sleep scientists, health advocates, and business groups raised conflicting concerns, arguing whether permanent daylight saving time or permanent standard time would be safer and healthier, particularly regarding dark morning commutes for schoolchildren.

Because the proposed legislation failed to pass both chambers before the legislative session expired, the measure effectively died. As a result, the federal mandate remains unchanged, and states continue to observe the time changes as they have for decades. Until lawmakers can bridge their differences and pass a unified bill that is signed into federal law, the seasonal shifting of clocks will remain an inescapable part of daily life.
