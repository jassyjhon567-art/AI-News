'Proof by Underpants' Provides a Novel Method to Test Soil Health

www.popsci.com · Wed, 26 Aug 2026 16:03:00 -0400

Researchers in Switzerland buried over 2,000 pairs of cotton underwear to engage citizen scientists and assess soil microbial health through fabric decomposition.

In an unusual blend of citizen science and soil biology, researchers in Switzerland launched an innovative project known as "Proof by Underpants" to evaluate the health of local soils. As part of the experiment, participants including farmers, gardeners, and citizen volunteers buried more than 2,000 pairs of identical white cotton underwear across various agricultural fields, forests, and private gardens throughout the country.

The underlying science behind the method is surprisingly straightforward. Cotton is composed primarily of cellulose, an organic material that serves as a prime food source for subterranean microbes, fungi, and earthworms. By leaving the garments buried underground for several months before unearthing them, scientists and participants can assess soil activity based on the degree of decomposition. Soils rich in biological diversity break down the fabric rapidly, leaving behind little more than the synthetic elastic waistband, whereas degraded soils leave the fabric largely intact.

Beyond generating valuable empirical data about regional soil quality, the study aims to raise awareness regarding the critical role of soil ecology. Healthy soils are vital for sustainable agriculture, carbon sequestration, and biodiversity preservation. By transforming complex environmental monitoring into an accessible and engaging hands-on activity, the project effectively highlights how everyday items can contribute to serious scientific discovery.
