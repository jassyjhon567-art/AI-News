2,400-Year-Old Tomb Discovered in Turkey May Belong to Ancient Wrestler

www.livescience.com · Mon, 10 Aug 2026 20:07:51 +0000

Archaeologists in Turkey have unearthed a 2,400-year-old tomb in Aspendos containing a skeleton buried with ancient coins depicting wrestlers, suggesting the grave belonged to a historic athlete.

Archaeologists conducting excavations in the ancient city of Aspendos, located in present-day southern Turkey, have unearthed a remarkable 2,400-year-old tomb that may have belonged to an ancient wrestler. The historic city, renowned for its remarkably preserved Roman theater and vibrant Classical history, continues to yield significant archaeological finds. However, this recent burial site has particularly captured the attention of researchers due to the intriguing personal items buried alongside the deceased human remains.

Within the ancient stone tomb, researchers discovered a well-preserved skeleton accompanied by several valuable artifacts, most notably a series of bronze and silver coins featuring depictions of two wrestlers engaged in combat. These coins, minted locally during the Classical period, strongly suggest that the individual held a deep connection to the sport, likely as a renowned champion, trainer, or honored patron. In ancient Greco-Roman culture, wrestling was celebrated not merely as entertainment, but as a discipline representing strength, honor, and elite social standing.

The unique discovery offers fascinating new insights into the burial customs and societal respect afforded to athletes in ancient Anatolia. Anthropologists and historians now plan to perform detailed osteological analyses on the skeletal remains to determine the individual's age at death, physical health, and evidence of repetitive physical trauma typical of combat sports. As excavations in Aspendos proceed, experts hope that additional contextual findings will help reconstruct the life story of this ancient athlete and further elucidate the cultural significance of athletics in the region.
