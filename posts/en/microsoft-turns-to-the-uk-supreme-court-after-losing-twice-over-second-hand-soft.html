Microsoft Appeals to UK Supreme Court in High-Stakes Second-Hand Software Dispute

www.techradar.com · Fri, 31 Jul 2026 07:00:00 +0000

Microsoft has appealed to the UK Supreme Court following two court defeats over second-hand software license resales, a case that could impact billions in software revenues.

Microsoft has officially taken its long-running legal battle regarding the resale of second-hand software licenses to the UK Supreme Court after suffering two consecutive losses in lower courts. The technology giant is seeking to overturn previous judicial rulings that granted third-party vendors the legal right to trade pre-owned software licenses. At the heart of the dispute is whether perpetual licenses for legacy products, such as Microsoft Office, can be lawfully transferred between corporate entities without infringing on copyright protections or existing licensing terms.

In its defense during earlier legal proceedings, Microsoft put forward a novel argument claiming that integrated elements, including embedded Office clip art, created a complex copyright structure that made the software unresellable. However, judges in two successive UK courts firmly rejected this position. The courts ruled that fundamental copyright principles, specifically the doctrine of exhaustion, dictate that once a software publisher sells a license, its right to control the subsequent resale of that specific copy is legally exhausted.

The impending judgment from the Supreme Court carries staggering financial ramifications, with billions of dollars potentially riding on the final outcome for both Microsoft and the broader software industry. Should the court uphold the lower decisions, it would firmly establish a legal framework for a thriving secondary software market in the United Kingdom, allowing organizations to purchase pre-owned licenses at significantly reduced costs. Conversely, a decision in Microsoft's favor could severely curtail the second-hand software market and dramatically shift the dynamics of digital software distribution.
