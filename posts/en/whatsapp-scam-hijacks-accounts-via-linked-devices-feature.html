Sophisticated WhatsApp Scam Hijacks Accounts via Linked Devices Feature

www.infosecurity-magazine.com · Tue, 04 Aug 2026 14:30:00 GMT

A novel WhatsApp scam exploits the platform's 'Linked Devices' feature to hijack user accounts without stealing passwords, allowing attackers continuous access to private messages and personal data.

Cybersecurity experts have issued an urgent warning regarding a sophisticated cyber scam that exploits WhatsApp's popular "Linked Devices" functionality to hijack user accounts without authorization. Unlike traditional cyberattacks that rely on stealing passwords or intercepting one-time verification codes, this novel method tricks individuals into granting access directly to malicious actors. Consequently, cybercriminals can covertly control accounts while leaving the primary owners completely oblivious to the ongoing security breach for extended periods.

The scheme typically begins with deceptive social engineering tactics, where victims receive fraudulent messages prompting them to scan malicious QR codes or visit compromised web pages. Once an unauthorized connection is successfully established through the messaging application's multi-device framework, perpetrators gain continuous, real-time access to private chat histories, personal media, and contact lists. Because secondary linked devices can remain logged in independently of the main smartphone, attackers can quietly monitor incoming messages, send scam requests to unsuspecting contacts, and harvest sensitive personal data.

To defend against this emerging threat, security analysts strongly urge all WhatsApp users to routinely review the active sessions listed under the "Linked Devices" section in their account settings. Any unrecognized or suspicious devices should be terminated immediately to revoke access. Furthermore, users are advised to exercise extreme caution when encountering unexpected QR codes, strictly avoiding unverified requests to maintain complete control over their messaging accounts and protect their digital privacy.
