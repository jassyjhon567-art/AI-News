Paul Blart's Revenge: How the Mocked Segway Laid the Foundation for Modern E-Scooters

www.popsci.com · Sat, 08 Aug 2026 08:00:00 -0400

Once mocked as a quirky failure, the Segway's groundbreaking self-balancing technology and engineering ultimately laid the foundation for today's widespread urban electric scooters.

When Dean Kamen introduced the Segway PT in 2001, it was hyped as a revolutionary invention that would redesign urban transportation. However, the bulky, expensive, two-wheeled self-balancing vehicle failed to capture the public imagination in the way its creators envisioned. Instead of replacing cars, the Segway quickly became the subject of pop-culture punchlines, most famously cemented in media as the preferred ride of comical security guards like Paul Blart. Critics argued that the device was simultaneously too ambitious in its technology and not ambitious enough in solving practical daily commute problems for average consumers.

Despite its failure to achieve mainstream dominance, the Segway laid critical technological groundwork that quietly powered the next mobility revolution. The engineering behind the device—specifically its advanced gyroscopic sensors, compact brushless motors, lightweight lithium-ion battery integration, and self-balancing software—was ahead of its time. As cities became more congested and battery technology matured, inventors and urban planners began rethinking short-distance travel. The core components developed for the once-mocked Segway were adapted to create simpler, lighter, and far more accessible personal electric vehicles.

Today, that legacy lives on in the ubiquitous electric scooters lining city sidewalks around the globe. After Beijing-based startup Ninebot acquired Segway in 2015, the combined company leveraged Segway's patents and engineering expertise to manufacture the majority of the world's shared e-scooters used by ride-hailing services. The dorky machine that once struggled for purpose ultimately provided the DNA for modern micromobility, transforming how millions of people navigate urban spaces every day and proving that its pioneering vision was simply waiting for the right form factor.
