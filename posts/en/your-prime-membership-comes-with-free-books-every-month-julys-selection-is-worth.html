Amazon Prime Members Reminded to Claim Their Free Monthly Books Before July Selection Expires

www.makeuseof.com · Wed, 29 Jul 2026 13:17:39 GMT

Amazon Prime members have just a few days left to claim up to two free Kindle eBooks for July through the monthly Amazon First Reads program before the offer expires.

Amazon Prime subscribers are being reminded to take full advantage of one of the service’s most valuable yet frequently overlooked benefits before the current month comes to an end. As part of the popular Amazon First Reads program, Prime members are eligible to download a selection of complimentary Kindle eBooks each month ahead of their official publication dates. With July rapidly drawing to a close, users have only a few days left to claim their free digital titles before the monthly offers expire and the catalog resets for August.

The First Reads initiative provides readers with exclusive early access to newly published works across a diverse range of genres, including suspenseful thrillers, contemporary romance, historical fiction, and insightful non-fiction. Each month, Amazon editors carefully curate a fresh list of upcoming releases, allowing subscribers to select up to two free eBooks to keep permanently in their personal digital libraries. Even members who do not own a dedicated Kindle e-reader can easily enjoy these books using the free Kindle app, which is accessible on smartphones, tablets, and web browsers.

While many subscribers primarily utilize Amazon Prime for fast shipping and video streaming, digital reading perks like First Reads offer significant extra value to the overall membership package. Claiming these monthly selections is entirely hassle-free; users simply visit the dedicated First Reads section on the Amazon website, pick their favorite titles, and download them at zero additional cost. Missing the upcoming deadline means forfeiting July’s specific selection, so book enthusiasts are encouraged to claim their titles promptly before the end-of-month offer officially expires.
