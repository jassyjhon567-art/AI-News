Baby T. Rex Were Born Predators, New Fossil Findings Suggest

www.nature.com · 2026-07-24

New fossil evidence reveals juvenile T. rex were born hunters, while recent research highlights the power of Egyptian princesses and London's rise as an AI safety capital.

Recent palaeontological discoveries suggest that juvenile Tyrannosaurus rex individuals were formidable predators right from a remarkably young age. Newly uncovered fossil evidence reveals that these young dinosaurs possessed specialized serrated teeth and lightweight, agile jaw structures specifically adapted for capturing fast-moving prey long before reaching full adulthood. Rather than relying entirely on parental provisioning or passive scavenging, these energetic juvenile carnivores actively hunted smaller animals, filling a distinct ecological niche and reshaping current scientific understanding of how Late Cretaceous food webs functioned.

In addition to prehistoric breakthroughs, recent historical research is illuminating the extraordinary influence and agency exercised by ancient Egyptian princesses. Re-examined historical inscriptions and archaeological artifacts demonstrate that these royal women were far more than passive figures within dynastic lineages. Instead, many served as pivotal leaders in administrative governance, religious diplomacy, and economic management. Their dynamic leadership and diplomatic prowess highlight a far more complex structure of royal power in ancient Egypt than previously acknowledged by traditional historical narratives.

Concurrently, modern technological developments emphasize the rapid rise of London as a leading global capital for artificial intelligence safety and governance. Supported by strategic public investments, international scientific partnerships, and world-class academic institutions, the city has become a major hub for evaluating and mitigating systemic risks tied to advanced machine learning systems. As governments and tech industry leaders work to establish comprehensive ethical standards, London's robust regulatory and research ecosystem is positioning the United Kingdom at the center of responsible technological innovation.
