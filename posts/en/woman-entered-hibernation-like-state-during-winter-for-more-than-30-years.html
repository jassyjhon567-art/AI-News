Medical Mystery: Woman Spent Decades Entering Winter Hibernation-Like State

www.newscientist.com · Thu, 19 Nov 2026 16:24:26 +0000

Researchers studying donated brains have uncovered a rare case of a woman who entered a hibernation-like state during winters for over thirty years, with temperatures dropping to 31°C.

While conducting examinations on human brains donated for neurological and scientific research, scientists uncovered an extraordinary medical anomaly involving an individual who appeared to experience periodic bouts of torpor. The researchers documented the unique case of an adult woman who, over a span of more than three decades, consistently entered prolonged states of sharply reduced physiological activity each year during the colder winter months.

During these recurring seasonal episodes, the woman's core body temperature would plunge significantly, on several occasions dropping as low as 31°C (87.8°F). Under ordinary circumstances, such an extreme drop in body heat would induce severe, life-threatening hypothermia and potentially fatal complications in the human body. Alongside this dramatic thermal cooling, her metabolic rate and physical responsiveness decreased markedly for weeks or months at a time. Yet, remarkably, she managed to emerge from these dormant cycles repeatedly throughout her life without sustaining the catastrophic systemic damage typically associated with such conditions.

True biological hibernation has long been considered virtually non-existent in humans, making this decades-long phenomenon profoundly fascinating for neuroscientists and medical investigators alike. While researchers continue to study controlled, induced hypothermia for practical applications like emergency trauma management and long-duration space travel, this singular occurrence presents intriguing clues about natural biological flexibility. Examining the brain mechanisms that enabled such dramatic metabolic deceleration may ultimately offer vital insights into therapeutic metabolic control and the untapped boundaries of human survival.
