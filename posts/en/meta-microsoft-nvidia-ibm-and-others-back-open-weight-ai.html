Tech Giants Join Forces to Urge US Policymakers to Protect Open-Weight AI

www.artificialintelligence-news.com · Fri, 24 Jul 2026 16:18:30 +0000

A coalition of over two dozen tech leaders, including Meta, Microsoft, and Nvidia, has signed an open letter urging US policymakers to protect open-weight artificial intelligence models.

A coalition of more than two dozen leading technology companies, venture capital firms, and research organizations has published an open letter urging United States policymakers to safeguard the development and distribution of open-weight artificial intelligence models. The signatory list features an unprecedented mix of industry giants and direct commercial rivals, including Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, IBM, Dell Technologies, and Palantir. They are joined by prominent AI pioneers and specialized firms such as Hugging Face, Perplexity, and Mistral, demonstrating a unified front despite their divergent commercial strategies.

The open letter stresses that open-weight models are crucial for fostering global technological innovation, advancing scientific research, and enhancing cybersecurity. Unlike closed proprietary systems, open-weight models allow independent researchers, academics, and software developers to inspect, audit, and customize foundational AI architectures. The coalition argues that imposing overly restrictive regulations on open-source and open-weight software could inadvertently concentrate market power among a small group of well-funded corporations, thereby stifling broader economic growth and limiting public access to cutting-edge tools.

As federal lawmakers and regulatory bodies in Washington consider new legislative frameworks to manage potential artificial intelligence risks, this coalition seeks to ensure that open innovation remains a key policy priority. Major venture capital firms like Andreessen Horowitz, alongside leading enterprise security and software providers like CrowdStrike and ServiceNow, have strongly backed the movement. Ultimately, the signatories urge policymakers to craft balanced regulations that address legitimate safety concerns without dismantling the collaborative open-source ecosystem that has historically driven software breakthroughs.
